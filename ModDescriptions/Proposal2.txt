PROPOSAL 2 - BEYOND THE SKY   (a space mod; MAJOR, on the scale of Seeker of the Dao; for approval - nothing is built)

The portal brought the first character to this world. This mod is about leaving it the hard way: a rocket built
stage by stage on a pad, a launch that can fail, an orbit, a station bolted together in the dark, the Moon, the
asteroid belt, a red planet with weather that kills, and the wreck of whoever came before.

It differs from Dimensions (which adds places you step into through a door) in what the places DO to you: there
is no air, gravity is not what it was, the cold and the sun are both deadly, and everything you need has to be
carried up or made there. The work of the mod is life support, rocketry and a base that keeps itself alive.

    a telescope  ->  a sounding rocket  ->  orbit  ->  a station  ->  the Moon  ->  the Belt  ->  the red planet  ->
    the derelict, and the engine that does not need fuel

Size: three or four times the base game in blocks and recipes; about the size of Seeker of the Dao.

================================================================================================================
0. QUESTIONS
================================================================================================================

Q1. HOW REAL. Recommended: the feel of rocketry without the arithmetic - a rocket has a mass and its engines a
    thrust and a burn time, and the game tells you plainly "reaches orbit" or "falls short by so much". Staging
    matters, payload matters, fuel matters; orbital mechanics do not. Alternative: launches as a recipe (build
    the rocket, click, you are there) - much less work, much less of a game.

Q2. WHAT A ROCKET IS. Recommended: built of BLOCKS on a launch pad - tanks, engines, a capsule, fairings,
    decouplers - stacked in a column up to five wide. The pad reads the stack (as a formation reads its flags)
    and works out what it will do. At launch the blocks become a vehicle (the engine has vehicles) and fly; what
    is jettisoned falls back and can be recovered or is lost. So rockets are designed, not chosen from a list.

Q3. AIR. The heart of it. Recommended: a room is breathable if it is SEALED (every way out is a wall, a window or
    an airlock) and has had air put in it. The game works out sealed rooms by flooding from each air vent. Break
    a wall and the air goes - quickly from a small room, slowly from a big one - and everything in it that needs
    air starts to die. Outside a sealed room: a suit, with a tank that runs down.

Q4. GRAVITY. Recommended: each place has its own - the Moon a sixth (jump six blocks, fall slowly, carry more),
    orbit none (you float; you move by pushing off walls and by a suit's thrusters; loose things drift), the red
    planet a third. This touches how characters move, which is the most delicate code in the game: say if you
    would rather gravity were only "low" and "normal", with no weightlessness.

Q5. WHERE THE PLACES ARE. The engine stacks other places under the world. Recommended: orbit, the Moon, the Belt
    and the red planet are four such places (each as wide as the home world, less tall), with the sky of each
    drawn for where it is - the home world hanging in the Moon's sky. Travel between them is by rocket only, and
    takes time (the game runs fast while you wait, as it does in sleep).

Q6. POWER. Space needs electricity for everything. Recommended: the mod brings its own small power set (solar
    panels, batteries, cables, a reactor) under its own names, and uses Industry's where Industry is on. It must
    not depend on Industry.

Q7. AN ENDING. Recommended: yes - the derelict ship at the edge of the red planet's sky, its drive repaired with
    the last tier of everything, and a last launch to "somewhere else" that rolls the credits and leaves the
    world playable. The game has no ending now; this would be the first.

Q8. DELIVERY. Recommended: six stages - (1) telescope, sounding rockets, the pad; (2) air, suits, sealed rooms,
    orbit and the station; (3) the Moon; (4) the Belt and mining ships; (5) the red planet; (6) the derelict.

================================================================================================================
1. ROCKETRY
================================================================================================================

THE PAD. A LAUNCH PAD with a GANTRY and a CONTROL DESK. Whatever is stacked on the pad is the rocket. The desk
shows it as a column with numbers: mass, thrust at each stage, burn time, and the verdict - which place it can
reach with what is aboard, and how much it has to spare.

PARTS, five tiers of each (one a place):
   ENGINES      solid motor (cheap, cannot be shut off); kerosene; hydrogen; methane made on the red planet;
                nuclear thermal; and the derelict's drive
   TANKS        fuel and oxidiser, in three widths; insulated for hydrogen
   CAPSULES     one seat, three seats, a lander with legs, a cargo pod, a probe core (no one aboard)
   STRUCTURE    decouplers, fairings, interstage, fins, landing legs, heat shield, parachutes, docking port
   PAYLOAD      satellite buses, station modules, rovers folded up, a habitat that unfolds

WHAT GOES WRONG. An engine has a reliability that rises each time its kind flies. A failure is a fire on the
pad, an engine out (the desk says whether the rest still make it), or a stage that will not separate. A LAUNCH
ESCAPE TOWER saves the crew. Test stands let an engine be run on the ground first. This is the mod's dice.

FUEL. Made, not found: an OIL WELL and REFINERY for kerosene; ELECTROLYSIS of water for hydrogen and oxygen
(which is also the air); a cryo plant; a SABATIER plant on the red planet. Pipes, pumps, a tank farm, a
fuelling arm on the gantry.

BEFORE ANYONE GOES. A telescope finds the places (they cannot be flown to until found). Sounding rockets bring
back samples of the upper air. PROBES map a landing site: without one, the lander comes down somewhere bad.
SATELLITES do things at home: a weather satellite forecasts, a survey satellite shows ore through the map, a
relay lets a terminal at home see a base on the Moon.

================================================================================================================
2. STAYING ALIVE
================================================================================================================

AIR (Q3): vents, tanks, scrubbers (a room's air goes bad as it is breathed, unless scrubbed or plants grow in
it), airlocks (two doors, never both open), pressure doors that shut by themselves when a room is holed, patch
kits, an alarm. A character's panel shows the air of the room they are in.

SUITS: five, from a pressure suit that is good for minutes to a hard suit good for a day; each with air, power,
heat and cold limits, a helmet lamp, thrusters in orbit, and slots for a tool belt. A suit is repaired, not
replaced; a torn one leaks.

HEAT AND COLD. The game already has an environment score. Here it is life or death: shade on the Moon is
colder than anything at home and sunlight hotter. Radiators, heaters, insulation, and the base buried under
regolith for the nights, which are two weeks long.

RADIATION, lightly: solar storms are forecast and must be sat out behind enough wall.

FOOD AND WATER. Carried at first. Then hydroponics (crops in trays under lamps, in a sealed room, cleaning the
air as they grow), a water recycler, ice mined at the Moon's pole, algae vats, and at last a closed loop: a base
that needs nothing from home. Getting there is the middle of the game.

================================================================================================================
3. THE PLACES
================================================================================================================

ORBIT. Black, the world turning below. Nothing to stand on but what is brought. The STATION is built of modules
launched one at a time and docked: hub, lab, habitat, greenhouse, solar truss, dock. Weightless inside. Outside,
on a tether. Things found there: dead satellites to salvage, micrometeorites that hole a module now and then.

THE MOON. Grey regolith over rock; craters with dark floors where ice is; lava tubes (ready-made caves, the
natural place for a base); the peaks at the pole where the sun never sets. Helium-3 in the dust, titanium,
rare glass. Low gravity makes building tall easy and falling slow. A mass driver throws cargo back to orbit.

THE BELT. Hundreds of asteroids as small floating bodies (the island generator, with no gravity between them):
metal ones, ice ones, carbon ones, and a few that are hollow, or are not rocks. Mined from a MINING SHIP that
latches on. The richest ore in the game, and the hardest place to be.

THE RED PLANET. Rust plains, a canyon deeper than the world is tall, dust storms that last days and bury solar
panels, dust devils, frost at dawn, a thin air that will not keep you alive but will make fuel. Caves with
something growing in them. The ruins of an earlier base - not yours.

THE DERELICT. Above the red planet. A ship the size of a village, dead and open to space, in sections that must
be sealed, powered and pumped up one by one - a dungeon whose danger is the place itself. Its logs, read in
order, are the mod's story. Its drive is the ending (Q7).

================================================================================================================
4. WHAT IT MAKES
================================================================================================================

Materials: aluminium, titanium alloys, composites, heat tiles, aerogel, glass that holds pressure, regolith
brick and sintered concrete, helium-3, platinum-group metals, ice, perchlorate, meteoric iron, things from the
derelict that cannot be made. Electronics in five tiers (boards, chips, sensors, computers, a flight computer).
Benches: clean room, machine shop, composites oven, cryo plant, electronics bench, assembly bay, test stand,
sample lab (what is brought back is studied there, and studying unlocks the next tier - the mod's research).
Machines: rovers (a vehicle that drives on ground, pressurised later), drills, a regolith sifter, 3D printers
that make blocks from local dust, robot arms, solar farms, a reactor, beamed power.
Creatures: almost none, and that is the point - until the red planet's caves, and the derelict.
LONG MAKINGS: a crewed Moon rocket is about fifteen makings deep; the closed-loop base and the drive repair
past twenty. Every tier's electronics are made with the tier's before.

================================================================================================================
5. ENGINE
================================================================================================================

New, as data fields:
   - gravity per place (and none): how far characters and creatures jump and fall, what floats;
   - sealed rooms and air: a flood-fill kept up to date as blocks change, air as a quantity per room, leaks;
   - a pattern-reader for a stack of blocks (the pad), and blocks that become a vehicle and back again;
   - vehicles that fly a set path between places, taking time; vehicles that drive on ground (rovers);
   - a suit as a worn thing with its own meters (air, power);
   - the environment score made lethal at its ends, with day lengths per place;
   - research: recipes that are known only once something has been studied (Seeker of the Dao's formulas, as
     a general rule);
   - a sky drawn per place (a planet in it, stars, no blue);
   - an ending.
Reused: other places (Dimensions' engine), vehicles (trains), power (Industry's fields), signals (alarms and
doors), structures (ruins, the derelict), effects, storage networks if Digital Storage is on.

HARD PARTS: weightless movement (Q4); keeping sealed rooms right when blocks change every tick; a rocket that
is blocks one moment and a vehicle the next; making a place with almost no creatures feel dangerous rather
than empty. None is impossible; the first is the one to prototype before anything else is promised.
