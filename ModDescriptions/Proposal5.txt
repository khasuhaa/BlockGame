PROPOSAL 5 - CABINET OF CURIOSITIES   (a digging-up and showing-off mod; medium; for approval - nothing is built)

The ground in this game is stone with ore in it. This mod puts a PAST in it: bones of things that lived here,
the rubbish and treasure of people who did, layer under layer - and then gives you somewhere to put what you
find. A museum that is yours, that is judged, that people come to see, and that is never finished.

The other mods give you things to make. This one gives you things to FIND, to piece together, to understand,
and to arrange - a collector's game, and a quieter one than anything else here.

Size: ~200 blocks, ~450 items (most of them finds), ~150 recipes, 12 creatures brought back from the dead.

================================================================================================================
0. QUESTIONS
================================================================================================================

Q1. HOW DIGGING WORKS. Recommended: a find is not dropped by a pickaxe - a pickaxe BREAKS it. A dig site is
    worked with a trowel and a brush: a small screen of the block's face, in layers, where you clear matrix
    from around a thing without striking the thing (a few taps too many and it cracks; cracked finds are worth
    less and show it). It takes a minute a find, and it is the mod's one new toy. Alternative: finds simply
    drop, with a chance of damage by the tool used. Much less work, and much less to it.

Q2. OLD WORLDS. Finds are put in the ground when a world is made. Recommended: a SURVEYOR'S STAKE that, set in
    any world, seeds dig sites in the unexplored ground around it, so that worlds made before the mod are not
    left out.

Q3. BRINGING THINGS BACK. A fossil in a case is one thing; a living one is another. Recommended: yes, late and
    hard - a few creatures can be raised from what is found, and kept. (Every creature in the game can be
    caged; these can too.)

Q4. WHO VISITS. Recommended: visitors are simple - they arrive by the portal, walk the halls, stop at what
    interests them, leave coins and a remark, and go. They are not settlers and need nothing but a way round.
    With Rags to Riches on, they come from the villages as well.

Q5. DELIVERY. Recommended: three stages - (1) dig sites, the trowel, fossils, the first cases;
    (2) the peoples and their things, restoration, the museum's score, visitors; (3) the living ones, the
    expeditions, the last room.

================================================================================================================
1. WHAT IS IN THE GROUND
================================================================================================================

STRATA. Depth is time. Each band of depth is an AGE, and each age has its own finds, told from the colour of
the dig-site block before it is touched:

   TOPSOIL          yesterday: lost tools, coins, bottles, a dodo's collar with a name on it
   THE SETTLERS     pottery, a plough, glass beads, a cellar with its jars still sealed
   THE BUILDERS     cut stone, bronzes, mosaics in pieces, a road under the road, tombs
   THE FIRST ONES   flint, bone needles, painted cave walls, a hearth, footprints
   THE ICE          mammoth, sabre-tooth, a great deer; a frozen one, whole, in the far north
   THE GIANTS       the big bones: twelve great beasts, each in forty to ninety pieces
   THE SEAS         shells, sea lilies, armoured fish, things with too many legs
   THE DEEP TIME    imprints in black shale, amber with something in it, and one thing that should not be here

About 450 finds. Each has a rarity, a condition (pristine, good, worn, cracked), and - for the made things - a
PEOPLE it belonged to: three vanished peoples, each with its own look, its own script, and its own end.

DIG SITES are found by the lie of the land (a mound, a dark band in a cliff, a sinkhole), by a DIVINING kit
that ticks faster near one, and by reading: a find sometimes points to another.

BIG FINDS. A giant's skeleton is spread through many blocks of one site. A tomb is rooms. A buried ship, a
kiln with its last firing in it, a library of clay tablets. These are expeditions, with a camp.

================================================================================================================
2. THE WORKSHOP
================================================================================================================

Nothing goes straight in a case.

   THE PREP TABLE     plaster jackets are opened, matrix taken off under a lamp. Condition can be raised by
                      one step with skill and patience, or lost.
   THE MENDING BENCH  pieces are joined: a pot from sherds, a mosaic from tesserae, a tablet from fragments.
                      A jigsaw, in effect - you hold some pieces and must find or trade for the rest. Gaps can
                      be filled with plain plaster (honest, and scored lower) or left.
   THE ARTICULATOR    bones become a skeleton on an armature, posed as you choose from a few poses. A giant
                      takes a hall. It can be mounted with pieces missing, and shows it.
   THE STUDY          the scripts. Every inscribed find adds signs to a table; with enough, a people's writing
                      can be READ, and then every tablet, tomb wall and pot mark already in the collection says
                      what it says. That is how the three peoples' stories are told - by you, in pieces, out
                      of order.
   THE CAST ROOM      copies. A cast can be shown where the real thing is too fragile, traded to another
                      collector, or sold.

IDENTIFYING. A find comes out of the ground as "a bone" or "a sherd". The study, reference finds already held,
and a growing CATALOGUE turn it into what it is. Discovery Road's relic identifier does something like this
for relics; here it is the middle of the game. A thing first identified in a world is named in the catalogue
as yours.

================================================================================================================
3. THE MUSEUM
================================================================================================================

Built of ordinary blocks, however you like. What makes it a museum is what is in it:

   CASES AND MOUNTS   glass cases in five sizes, plinths, wall mounts, floor mosaics under glass, a diorama
                      case (a little scene with a painted back), frames, a great hall armature for a giant
   LABELS             every exhibit has one. The game writes what is known; you may add a line.
   LIGHT              an exhibit is judged by how it is lit (not dim, not in the sun), and some things fade.
   ROOMS              a room with a THEME - one age, one people, one kind of beast - scores more than the
                      same things scattered. A room's theme is worked out from what is in it and shown on a
                      plaque by the door.

THE SCORE. Each exhibit: what it is, how rare, what condition, how complete, how shown. Each room: its theme
and how nearly it tells the whole of it. The museum: all of them, and how the rooms follow one another. It is
shown as a number and as STARS, and - more usefully - as a line under each thing saying what would make it
better.

VISITORS (Q4). More come as the stars rise. They leave coin (the mod's income: slow, steady, and the reason
the museum pays for the next dig) and remarks ("I walked straight past the sea-lilies: is that room lit?").
Now and then: a scholar who will trade a find you lack for a cast of one you have; a rival collector with an
offer; a child who asks what the thing in the last room is.

SETS AND GAPS. The catalogue shows every find there is as a silhouette until it is found (as the trade menu
shows a ? until an item is found). Complete a people's set, an age's, a beast's: each completion gives
something - a recipe, a tool, a room plaque in gold. Nothing in the game is as long as filling the last gap.

================================================================================================================
4. THE LIVING ONES
================================================================================================================

Late. From amber, from the frozen one, from eggs in a nest in the Giants' stratum: twelve creatures that can
be raised in a HATCHERY, each wanting an enclosure of its own kind (a fern house, a cold pen, a deep tank) and
feed that must be grown from seeds that were also dug up. Small ones first - a thing like a chicken with
teeth - and at the end one of the giants, which wants a paddock the size of a village and does not like fences.
An enclosure is an exhibit: it is scored, and it is what brings the crowds.

And the last room. The one thing in the Deep Time that should not be there is in pieces across the whole
world, one piece to an age. Assembled, mended, read and put on its mount, it does something. It is the mod's
only secret and its ending.

================================================================================================================
5. WHAT IT MAKES
================================================================================================================

Tools: trowels and brushes in the game's metals, sieves, plaster and jackets, a divining kit, a surveyor's
level, a field notebook, a hand lens, dental picks (the finest work), a dig tent and camp bed, crates.
Building: display glass, velvet rope, museum floor and wall in six finishes, skylights that light without
fading, benches, a ticket desk, a donation box, banners, plaques.
Benches: the five of the workshop, a carpenter's for cases, a glazier's.
LONG MAKINGS: a mounted giant is its ninety bones found, prepared, some mended, an armature forged, a hall
built and lit - not a recipe at all, and the longest making in the game.

================================================================================================================
6. ENGINE
================================================================================================================

New, as data fields:
   - dig sites: blocks that hold a find chosen when the world is made (by depth, biome and a table), opened by
     a small excavation screen rather than broken;
   - condition and completeness kept with an item (a number, as meters are), and items made of named parts;
   - a jigsaw/assembly bench: a thing built from particular pieces, some of which may be missing;
   - identified and unidentified forms of an item, and a catalogue kept with the playset;
   - exhibits: a block that shows the item put in it, with a label, and can be scored;
   - rooms and their themes (a flood-fill; the same one a settlers mod or a space mod would want);
   - a scoring rule in data, with the reasons for a score kept so that they can be shown;
   - visitors: simple walkers with a path and a few lines to say;
   - big skeletons: a model made of many placed parts, posed.
Reused: structures (tombs, buried things), relic identifying (Discovery Road), cages and creatures, sets,
the trade menu's "?" for the unfound, signs (labels), light, the environment score.

HARD PARTS: an excavation screen that is a pleasure the four-hundredth time (Q1) - it needs variety by stratum
and by tool, or an auto-dig for what you have dug before; a score that rewards taste rather than hoarding;
450 finds that are worth looking at, which is the art of the whole mod and most of its work.
