QUESTBOOK   (a quest mod; asked for and built in one go - this is the record, not a proposal)

ASKED FOR. A questbook like the ones in Minecraft modpacks: it detects that a task is done, and rewards are claimed
from the book. An API other mods can use - but with the questbook not enabled, the other mods still work. A decent
questline of 100+ quests for each major content mod and about 30+ for each of the others. Tabs: a tab for each mod
that is on.

DECIDED WHERE NOTHING WAS SAID
   - THE API IS A FILE. A mod brings quests by having a quests.json beside its other files. The engine reads it
     always; only the Questbook mod (one rule: "questbook": true) makes it mean anything. So a mod needs nothing
     of the Questbook mod, and is exactly as it was with the book off. This is the game's rule for everything a
     mod does - through the data - and no mod depends on another.
   - WHICH MODS ARE MAJOR: Dimensions, Seeker of the Dao, Beyond the Sky, Plunder & Thunder, Dungeoneering.
     The other ten have about thirty each; the game itself has a tab of sixty.
   - QUESTS ARE THE WORLD'S, not a character's: whoever is played does them and claims them.
   - REWARDS ARE COIN, more the further along; three times as much for the first quest of a chapter and for
     the big things.
   - THE BOOK IS A BUTTON under the crystals, not an item and not a key.
   - A QUEST'S ORDER: chapters of eight; each chapter waits on the first quest of the one before; a thing made
     waits also on the quests for what it is made from, where those have quests. A chapter never waits on a
     matter of luck (a legendary, a relic).

BUILT
   - The engine: quests.json (tabs and quests), seven kinds of task the game sees done - have, kill, near, in,
     biome, stage, and check (ticked by hand) - locking, claiming one or all, saved with the world by name
     (save format 40).
   - The book: tabs with their counts, the list of the open tab, the quest chosen - its text, its task and how
     far it has got, what it comes after, what it gives - and Claim.
   - 966 quests in 16 tabs, written by tools/quests.py from each mod's own data: 128 for Dungeoneering, 112
     each for the other four big mods, 33 for each of the ten others, 60 for the game.

NOT BUILT
   - Quests written by hand. Every one is worked out from the data: it is "have this", "defeat these", "stand
     by this bench", "reach this place", and its text says what the thing is made from or where it is found.
     They follow each mod from its first bench to its last making, but nobody chose them. For the big mods a
     hand-written line through the generated one would be the next thing worth doing.
   - More than one task to a quest; a choice of rewards; rewards that are not items.
   - The quests laid out as a tree, as modpack questbooks have them.
   - Looked at in one screenshot, with four tabs. Not played.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE SECOND PASS   (the owner sent the first back)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SAID: it should not be linear quests but a tree, like a modpack's - tier 1 pills, then tier 2 pills, then tier 3,
where "tier 1 pills" unlocks all the tier 1 pill quests. And the rewards should not just be coins: get creative.

DONE
   - A tab is a tree on a page that is dragged about. A branch for each kind of thing a mod has (benches,
     creatures, places, and each kind of item - pills, scriptures, artifacts, loot, cargo, tools ...); stages
     along the branch; a head to each stage ("Pills II"), waiting on the head before it; every other quest of
     the stage hanging from its head. The chapters of eight are gone.
   - More quests, so that the branches have something on them: about 160 for each big mod (198 for
     Dungeoneering), 40 for each of the others, 80 for the game - 1,318 in all.
   - Rewards by what the quest was: part of the makings back for a thing made; what is worked at a bench for
     setting it down; more of what a creature leaves for hunting it; food for finding a place. Heads give a
     CHOICE of one - coin, or what the next stage's makings want, or something to eat or drink from the mod -
     and crystals besides. A dungeon's boss gives the choice of a Rare warrior's or a Rare mage's weapon of
     that dungeon. Nothing is given from further along than the quest itself.
   - In the engine: a quest's place on the page, heads, "pick" and crystals.

STILL NOT
   - Written by hand: the branches and stages are worked out from each mod's categories and tiers.
   - Lines across branches: a thing made waits only on its stage's head, not on the quests for its makings.
   - Zoom. Looked at in two screenshots (Seeker of the Dao, Dungeoneering); not played.
