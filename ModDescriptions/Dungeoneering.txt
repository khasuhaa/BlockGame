DUNGEONEERING - PROPOSAL   (a dungeon-and-loot mod; MAJOR; for approval - nothing below is built)

Ten dungeons, one behind each tier of portal. You go in, fight room by room to a boss, and come out with
LOOT: weapons, armour and spells with numbers on them, rolled when they drop, in five grades from Common to
Legendary. Better numbers let you clear the dungeon faster, then on a harder setting, then the next dungeon.
And each dungeon drops the stone that raises your portal to the next tier - so the dungeons ARE the way up
from Portal I to Portal X, for anyone who would rather fight for an amethyst than dig for one.

    Portal I: the Rat Cellars  ->  amethysts  ->  Portal II: the Drowned Crypt  ->  sapphires  ->  ...  ->
    Portal X: the Throne of Ash, and gear with numbers ten thousand times the first sword's

The model is Dungeon Quest: short runs, a chest at the end, a number that goes up. Two classes, WARRIOR and
MAGE, told apart by what they wear and wield, not chosen from a menu.

(Proposal3, "The Undercroft", is also dungeons. That one is about fighting as a SKILL - reach, block, dodge,
telegraphs - in mazes that are different every time. This one keeps the game's fighting as it is and is about
LOOT and numbers. They could share rooms and enemies. They should not both be built as they stand: say which.)

Size aimed at: ~260 blocks, ~900 items (about 700 of them loot), ~350 recipes, ~110 enemies, 10 bosses,
10 dungeons, each in four difficulties.

================================================================================================================
0. QUESTIONS
================================================================================================================

Q1. PORTALS VII TO X. The game's own portal stops at VI (amethyst, sapphire, emerald, ruby, diamond raise it).
    Dimensions, Seeker of the Dao and Beyond the Sky each add VII to X with stones of their own, and a mod may
    not depend on another. Recommended: this mod adds its own VII to X as well, raised by four KEYSTONES that
    only its dungeons VI to IX drop. So with nothing else on, the dungeons alone take a portal from I to X.
    Dungeons I to V drop the game's own gems. Dungeon X drops no stone: it is the top.
    Alternative: stop at six dungeons. Much less to it, and not what was asked.

Q2. WHERE A DUNGEON IS. Two ways:
      A. A PLACE OF ITS OWN, made new each time you go in and thrown away when you leave - a real instance,
         different every run. The engine cannot do this yet: its other places are kept for good and saved.
         It is the larger half of the engine work.
      B. ONE KEPT PLACE for each dungeon, made once like any dimension, whose rooms REFILL (enemies, chest)
         each time you enter by the door. Far less work; the layout is the same every run in that world.
    Recommended: A. A dungeon you can memorise is a corridor. But B first is a fair way to get there.

Q3. HOW YOU GET IN. Recommended: a DUNGEON GATE, a block made at the workbench (it is a thing to travel by,
    as portals are), set down beside a portal. It offers the dungeons up to that portal's tier and no higher.
    Click it, pick the dungeon and the difficulty, go. Leaving is by the boss's door or by dying.

Q4. DYING. Recommended: you are put outside the gate, alive, with everything you brought - and nothing from
    that run. Loot is only yours when you walk out with it. A HARDCORE difficulty where what is worn is lost
    too is there for those who want it, and pays double.

Q5. NUMBERS ON ITEMS. The game's items are all alike: one iron sword is every iron sword. This mod needs each
    dropped item to carry its own rolled numbers. Recommended: one number kept with the stack (as a meter
    is) that is the SEED of the roll - grade, power, health and upgrades all come from it and from the item's
    definition, so nothing else in a save changes. Rolled items do not stack. Say if you would rather loot had
    fixed numbers per item and grade (five sword definitions a tier): no engine work, and no reason to run a
    dungeon twice.

Q6. HOW BIG THE NUMBERS GET. Dungeon Quest's numbers grow without shame, and that is half its charm.
    Recommended: each tier about 2.6 times the last, so tier X is some 5,000 times tier I (table in section
    3). The base game's own creatures and tools stay as they are: dungeon gear is strong outside dungeons,
    and that is the reward. Alternative: flatter (1.6 a tier), so that dungeon gear sits beside other mods'
    endgame gear instead of over it. Say which matters more.

Q7. WARRIOR AND MAGE. Recommended: no class is chosen. A weapon is a WARRIOR'S (physical power) or a MAGE'S
    (spell power); armour gives health and a little of one power or the other. Wear what you like; mixed
    sets work badly because each ability scales with one power only. A character can keep a set of each.
    Alternative: pick a class at the gate and be barred from the other's gear.

Q8. LEVELS. Dungeon Quest gates gear by character level. Recommended: yes, a DUNGEON LEVEL per character,
    1 to 100, gained only in dungeons; gear has a level it needs; ten levels a tier. It is what stops a
    Legendary from dungeon V being handed to a new character. Without it, the portal tier alone gates.

Q9. ALONE OR TOGETHER. A world may have three characters. Recommended: one goes in, for now. A party that
    follows and fights by rule is the Undercroft's hard problem and is left out here.

Q10. DELIVERY. Recommended: four stages, each playable -
      (1) the gate, dungeons I to III, rolled loot in five grades, the chest, selling and upgrading;
      (2) abilities, dungeon levels, difficulties, dungeons IV to VI and the first keystone;
      (3) dungeons VII to X, portals VII to X, legendaries with powers of their own;
      (4) the endless one, enchanting, cosmetics, the board of best runs.
    Or one sitting, as the last three mods were - with the same result: built wide, cut where it was hard,
    and unplayed. Say which.

================================================================================================================
1. A RUN
================================================================================================================

Click the gate. Choose a dungeon and a difficulty. You arrive in the first room.

A dungeon is a string of ROOMS - eight to fourteen of them - joined by doors that are shut until the room is
cleared. Each room is a fight: enemies come in WAVES, from the doors and from the floor. Between rooms:
corridors with traps, a side room now and then with a small chest, a shrine that heals once.

The last room is the BOSS. The boss has a name, a health bar across the top of the screen, and three or four
things it does, each with a warning: the floor it is about to hit goes red first. Kill it and the door behind
it opens on the TREASURE ROOM: one great chest, and a way out.

The chest gives: gold; three to six pieces of LOOT (section 3); this dungeon's portal stone, sometimes
(section 5); and dungeon experience. Then you are home, and the run is over. Ten to fifteen minutes.

DIFFICULTIES, each opened by clearing the one before:

   EASY         enemies as written                         grades up to Rare
   HARD         3x health, 2x damage, more waves           grades up to Epic; stone more likely
   INSANE       10x health, 4x damage, elite enemies       Legendary can drop; stone certain
   NIGHTMARE    30x health, 8x damage, every room elite    Legendary more likely; two stones; best rolls
   (HARDCORE    any of the above, one life, worn gear at stake: everything doubled.)

A dungeon's Nightmare is about as hard as the next dungeon's Easy, and drops a little worse. So there are
always two sensible things to run.

================================================================================================================
2. THE TEN DUNGEONS
================================================================================================================

Each has its own blocks, its own rooms, a family of about ten enemies, a boss, and a look that can be told
from across the room.

    tier  dungeon               what it is                                   boss                    drops for the portal
    I     The Rat Cellars       cellars, barrels, sewers                     The Rat King            amethyst
    II    The Drowned Crypt     flooded tombs, the walking dead              The Lich of the Locks   sapphire
    III   The Thornwood Deep    roots, spores, beasts, poison                The Elder Stag          emerald
    IV    The Ember Forge       lava, anvils, fire imps and iron golems      Forgemaster Ghol        ruby
    V     The Frozen Keep       ice halls, knights, breath that slows        The Pale Queen          diamond
    VI    The Sunken Temple     serpent-folk, falling water, moving floors   Naga Matriarch          tidestone     (keystone VII)
    VII   The Clockwork Vault   brass, pistons, sentries that never tire     The Regulator           gearstone     (keystone VIII)
    VIII  The Pirate Hold       (if liked) a hold of ghosts and gunpowder    Captain No-Tide         stormstone    (keystone IX)
    IX    The Star Observatory  glass, gravity that turns, mages             The Astronomer          voidstone     (keystone X)
    X     The Throne of Ash     everything before it, and a king             The Ashen King          nothing: the crown

ENEMIES, in every family: a grunt, a fast one, a ranged one, a tank, a healer or summoner, a bomber, a swarm,
and an ELITE of each (bigger, a coloured glow, one extra trick, better drops). About 110 kinds in all. Their
health and damage are numbers of the tier, not of the base game: a cellar rat has 40 health and a king's
guard two million.

BOSSES do things that must be walked out of, not just out-numbered: a charge along a line, a ring that
closes, adds that must be killed first, a floor that goes in quarters. On Insane and above each has one more.

TRAPS: spikes, darts, flame jets, crushers, floors that fall. Machinations has pistons and signals; this mod
uses the same fields under names of its own and does not need it on.

THE ENDLESS ONE (stage 4). A gate setting past X: floor after floor, each a room from any dungeon, each
harder by a fixed share. How deep you got is kept. It drops tier X loot with better rolls the deeper it goes.

================================================================================================================
3. LOOT
================================================================================================================

WHAT DROPS

   WARRIOR      greatsword, sword and shield, axe, hammer, spear          physical power
   MAGE         staff, wand and tome, orb, scythe                         spell power
   ARMOUR       helmet, chest, legs, boots - a warrior's (plate) and a mage's (robe) line each tier:
                health, and some of its class's power
   ABILITIES    spell and skill items (section 4): two worn at once
   RINGS        the relic slots: small flat bonuses and odd ones

Each dungeon has its own named gear - about 9 weapons, 2 armour sets and 6 abilities - so about 70 things a
dungeon and 700 in all, every one a picture of its own.

GRADES, by colour, on the item's name and its frame everywhere it is shown:

   COMMON       grey      1.00 x the dungeon's base      most of what drops
   UNCOMMON     green     1.15 x
   RARE         blue      1.35 x
   EPIC         purple    1.65 x                         Hard and above
   LEGENDARY    gold      2.10 x, and a POWER            Insane and above; about 1 chest in 60, 1 in 25 on Nightmare

Within a grade the number is ROLLED: 85% to 115% of the grade's figure. A good roll of a Rare beats a bad
roll of an Epic by a little, which is why a chest is always worth opening. The item shows its roll.

A LEGENDARY'S POWER is a rule of its own, one per item, named on it: "every fifth blow strikes twice",
"your first spell in a room costs nothing", "at one health, once a run, you do not die". Sixty of them.

THE NUMBERS (Q6, at 2.6 a tier; Common, mid roll):

    tier   weapon power    armour health (a piece)    an Easy grunt's health    the boss's health
    I             12                  40                         40                    1,500
    II            30                 100                        110                    4,000
    III           80                 270                        280                   10,000
    IV           210                 700                        740                   27,000
    V            550               1,800                      1,900                   70,000
    VI         1,400               4,700                      5,000                  180,000
    VII        3,700              12,000                     13,000                  470,000
    VIII       9,700              32,000                     34,000                1,200,000
    IX        25,000              83,000                     88,000                3,200,000
    X         65,000             220,000                    230,000                8,300,000

Big numbers are shown short: 1.2M, 65K.

UPGRADING. Any piece of loot can be upgraded with gold at the FORGE OF FORTUNE: each upgrade adds a fixed
share of its rolled number, the cost rises each time, and a piece takes as many upgrades as its grade allows
(Common 10, Uncommon 15, Rare 20, Epic 30, Legendary 45). Fully upgraded it is worth about 1.6 times what it
dropped as. Upgrades stay with the item.

SELLING. Loot sells for gold by tier, grade and upgrades - at the forge, at once, in bulk ("sell all Common").
Most of what drops is sold: that is where the gold for upgrades comes from. Dungeon loot is not dealt in at
trade portals.

SETS. A dungeon's plate set and its robe set each give something for 2 and 4 pieces, in the game's own way.

================================================================================================================
4. FIGHTING, WITH NUMBERS
================================================================================================================

The game's fighting is kept: click a creature to strike it with what is in hand. What changes is what a
blow is worth, and what else there is to press.

   PHYSICAL POWER   what a warrior's weapon and skills do
   SPELL POWER      what a mage's weapon and spells do
   HEALTH           the character's health becomes a number of the tier while in a dungeon: what is worn
                    adds up, and the bar at the top shows it. Outside, the game's own health is as it was,
                    and dungeon armour counts as armour in proportion.

WARRIOR'S WEAPONS strike what is next to you; some reach two squares, some hit everything in an arc. A
shield takes a share off every blow. MAGE'S WEAPONS throw a bolt at what is clicked, at range, for less,
and the mage is worth less health. That is the whole difference, and it is enough: one stands in it, one
does not.

ABILITIES. An ability is an item worn in one of two ABILITY SLOTS (new, beside the relic slots) and used by a
key (Q and E by default). Each has a cooldown shown on its slot, scales with one power, and has a grade and a
roll like any loot.

   a warrior's:  whirlwind, ground slam, charge, war cry (those near strike harder), taunt and guard, leap
   a mage's:     fireball, chain lightning, ice nova (slows), meteor, blink, mend (the only heal)

Six a dungeon, sixty in all, each tier's a stronger thing and not a bigger copy: tier I has a fireball,
tier IX a sun.

DUNGEON LEVEL (Q8). Experience from kills and from the chest. Each level: a little health, and a point to
put into POWER, HEALTH or STAMINA (cooldowns). Points can be taken back for gold. Gear needs a level.

================================================================================================================
5. THE PORTAL
================================================================================================================

Dungeon N is opened by a portal of tier N and drops the stone that makes it tier N+1.

   - I to V: the game's own gems, the same items that are mined. One amethyst raises a portal; the Rat
     Cellars give one on about every third Easy run, every Hard run, and two on Nightmare.
   - VI to IX: the keystones of Q1, found nowhere else, and the mod's own portals VII to X that take them.
   - Spare stones are not waste: the forge takes them for the last few upgrades of that tier's gear.

Trade portals follow the same tiers, as they do in the other mods that add them.

So the mod is a second road up the portal: slower than a lucky dig at tier I, the ONLY road past VI with
no other mod on, and one of several with them on.

================================================================================================================
6. AT HOME
================================================================================================================

   THE DUNGEON GATE        section 0, Q3. At the workbench.
   THE FORGE OF FORTUNE    upgrade, sell, and - stage 4 - REFORGE: spend a stone to roll a piece again within
                           its grade. Five grades, one for every two tiers.
   THE ARMOURY STAND       holds one whole outfit - weapon, armour, abilities, rings - and swaps it with what
                           you have on at a click. One for the warrior's, one for the mage's.
   THE TROPHY WALL         a boss's head for each dungeon cleared, in the difficulty's colour.
   THE RUN BOARD           each dungeon and difficulty: cleared or not, best time, deaths. Kept with the
                           playset. The Endless one's deepest floor.
   THE LOOT CHEST          storage that sorts by tier and grade and shows each piece's number.

Things to make, not find (the ~350 recipes): the benches above and their grades; potions for a run (heal,
power, haste: brewed from what dungeon enemies drop); keys that open a side vault in a dungeon; dungeon
blocks for building with, each dungeon's wall, floor, door, torch and furniture; cosmetics - cloaks and
auras that do nothing.

================================================================================================================
7. ENGINE
================================================================================================================

New, as data fields any mod could use:
   - ROLLED ITEMS (Q5): a seed kept with a stack; grade, roll and upgrades worked out from it; a grade's
     colour on the name and frame; items that do not stack because of it. This touches the inventory, the
     bar, containers, tooltips and saving, and is the first thing to build.
   - INSTANCES (Q2): a place made on entering from rooms joined by rule, not saved, thrown away on leaving.
     Rooms are structures. Doors that open when a room's enemies are dead. Waves.
   - POWER and big health: a character's attack and health as numbers set by what is worn while in such a
     place; damage shown as it lands; numbers shown short.
   - ABILITY SLOTS and cooldowns; abilities by key. (The game has spells that are cast from the hand, and
     Seeker of the Dao has techniques on Shift and a number: this is a third row, and should be made the
     same row.)
   - BOSSES: a creature with a health bar across the screen and attacks that mark their squares first.
     Enemy kinds as data (keeps away, heals others, explodes, summons) - the Undercroft asked for the same.
   - A level and points for a character, kept in the save.
   - The gate's menu, the forge's menu, the run board.
Reused: other places (Dimensions), structures, `"extends"` for portal tiers, relic slots and sets, effects
(run potions, slows, burns), cast spells, signals and pistons (traps), loot tables.

HARD PARTS, honestly:
   - Rolled items go through every place an item is handled. It is the mod, and it is not small.
   - A run must be fun the fortieth time: that is room variety, and room variety is hand work - about
     twenty rooms a dungeon, two hundred in all.
   - Bosses whose attacks can be read and walked out of in a game moved by clicking.
   - Ten tiers of numbers that stay in step: one table drives health, damage, gold and drop rates, so that
     it can be tuned in one place. It will still need playing, which none of the last three mods has had.
   - 700 loot pictures that are not recolours. The owner has said look-alikes of different stuff must not
     be one picture in ten colours; with ten tiers of sword that is the art of the whole mod.

   Dungeoneering              ~260     ~900     ~350      ~110       10        10      ~200       60

WHAT WAS DECIDED, WHAT WAS BUILT, AND WHAT WAS NOT   (October 2026)
================================================================================================================

THE OWNER'S ANSWERS. Q1: yes - the mod's own portals VII to X. Q2: yes - a dungeon made new each time. Q5: random
ranges: a base and a base count of upgrades, each give or take a tenth. And a drop table was supplied ("Copy of
Personal Min_Max List 2025"), of which the first ten dungeons were to be used. The other questions were not
answered; what was done about each is below.

THE DROP TABLE CHANGED THE PLAN. The dungeons are the table's ten - Desert Temple, Winter Outpost, Pirate Island,
King's Castle, The Underworld, Samurai Palace, The Canals, Ghastly Harbor, Steampunk Sewers, Orbital Outpost - not
the ten proposed in section 2, and the numbers are the table's, not section 3's: a first dagger of 6, a last
legendary of 3,150,000. Grades are the table's four and Legendary. Each thing's base and count of upgrades are the
table's averages; what an upgrade adds is worked out from the table's "potential".

BUILT (tools/mod_dungeoneering.py; tests in test_dungeoneering_mod)
   - The gate, reaching as far down the list as the portal beside it has been raised; four difficulties.
   - Dungeons built afresh on every entry (Q2): rooms in a row, sealed doors that open when a room is cleared,
     creatures that appear as you near, a boss with its health across the screen, a chest, a way out.
   - 1,054 rows of the table as 1,306 graded things (armour is a helm and a body piece), each with its own
     picture by what its name says it is and is made of. Numbers that fall when found, give or take a tenth (Q5).
   - Blows as numbers against a health that is 100 and what is worn.
   - The forge: upgrade by goes, upgrade all, sell.
   - The stone for the next portal tier in each chest; keystones and portals VII to X (Q1).
   - Six creatures a dungeon, the last its boss: 60, all cageable. Each dungeon's own wall, floor, lamp and
     furnishings, and the same to build with at home.
   SIZE, AS BUILT: 135 blocks, 1,472 items, 82 recipes, 60 creatures, 10 dungeons - against ~260 / ~900 / ~350 /
   ~110 proposed.

NOT BUILT, AND THE QUESTIONS NOT ANSWERED
   - Q3 (how you get in): as recommended.
   - Q4 (dying): you come back where the world began with what you brought; nothing of the run is yours, since
     it is all in the chest. No hardcore setting.
   - Q6 (how big the numbers get): settled by the table.
   - Q7 (classes): by what is held, as recommended - but ordinary armour's power adds to either class's weapon.
   - Q8 (levels): not built. Nothing but the portal's tier gates a dungeon, and all four difficulties are open
     from the first.
   - Q9: one character at a time, as recommended.
   - Q10: one sitting.
   - Abilities, ability slots and cooldowns (section 4): none.
   - Bosses that warn of their attacks; enemy kinds that heal, summon or explode; elites; traps; side rooms;
     shrines; keys. A boss is a large creature with a heavy blow up close and another from afar.
   - Rooms drawn by hand: they are laid out by rule.
   - Legendaries' own powers, rings, sets, reforging, the armoury stand, trophies, the run board, the loot
     chest that sorts, potions for a run, cosmetics, the endless dungeon.
   - It has not been played. Each dungeon's creatures are set against the best of the dungeon before - a blow
     of six tenths of its best weapon, a health of 100 and twice its best ordinary armour - and that is
     arithmetic, not balance.
