HULL & HORIZON - PROPOSAL   (a ship-building mod; a TOTAL CONVERSION; for approval - nothing below is built)

Turn the mod on and there is no land, no ground to dig and nothing of the game you know. There is a sea that
never stops going by, and in the middle of it two planks lashed together with you standing on them.

Everything you will ever have comes to you over the water or up from under it, and everything you make goes
onto the thing you are standing on. Two planks become a raft with a sail made of a shirt. The raft becomes a
boat with a keel. The boat becomes a ship with a hold under the waterline, then one with three masts and a gun
deck, then one with no masts at all: steel, funnels, turrets, a searchlight sweeping black water.

    a raft  ->  a sailboat  ->  a caravel  ->  a galleon  ->  a dreadnought

The ship never goes anywhere. The sea does. That is the whole trick of the mod, and why the engine can do it.

(Plunder & Thunder is also about ships, and is nothing like this. There a ship is a creature you ride round a
world of islands. Here the ship IS the world: it is made of blocks, you live in it, and it is the only place
there is. Nothing of that mod is used and the two cannot be on together - see Q1.)

Size aimed at: ~380 blocks, ~850 items, ~1,000 recipes, ~60 creatures, 5 keels, 6 seas, ~70 things that come
alongside. Great makings ten and more deep (a galleon's keel 10, a dreadnought's 15), enforced in the generator
as Seeker of the Dao's are.

================================================================================================================
0. QUESTIONS
================================================================================================================

Q1. HOW THE BASE GAME GOES AWAY. Recommended: a mod may say `"standalone": true`. With such a mod on, `data/` is
    NOT loaded under it - the mod's own files are the whole game: its own blocks, items, recipes, creatures,
    rules, trade categories and coins, world generator and quests. The game's pictures stay within reach (a mod
    may already name a texture of the game's), so its planks, water, glass and tools can be drawn with the art
    there is. A standalone mod has a playset to itself: no other mod can be on with it, because every other mod
    names things of the base game that would not be there. The mod list says so when it is ticked and turns the
    rest off for that playset; it does not touch any other playset or any world.
    Alternative, NOT recommended because it is not what was asked: the sea as a place reached from an ordinary
    world, like one of Dimensions'. Then pickaxes and iron come aboard on the first day and there is no raft.

Q2. WHAT MOVES. Recommended: the ship stands still in the grid and the sea is drawn going past - swell, foam at
    the bow, a wake, clouds and a far horizon at their own speeds, all quicker as the ship makes more way. Things
    in the water really do move: they drift in from ahead, pass down the side and are gone astern. So "speed" is
    real: more sail, more things an hour. The engine cannot move a piece of the world, and does not have to.
    The world is small - 384 squares wide and 160 tall, a sixteenth of an ordinary one - which is cheap.

Q3. WHAT MAKES A BOAT A SAILBOAT. Recommended: the KEEL. Each stage is a keel laid at the shipwright's bench,
    and a keel is a build envelope: so long, so high above the water, so deep below it. Inside the envelope you
    build as you like, block by block; outside it nothing can be placed. The keel is laid under the ship you
    have - nothing is taken down, the room simply gets bigger. A keel is the great making of its stage.
    Alternative: no keels - the ship is measured, and is a galleon when it is big enough. Freer, but then there
    is no great thing to work toward, and a hundred-block raft is the quickest way to everything.

Q4. THE STAGE BETWEEN. You asked for one between sailboat and galleon. Recommended: the CARAVEL - two masts, the
    first deck below the waterline, the first guns, the first ship that can anchor and the first that can leave
    sight of where it began. Others that would do: a cog, a brig, a schooner. Say which name you like.

Q5. WEIGHT. Recommended, simple: hull blocks give BURDEN, everything else aboard costs some, and the shipwright's
    bench shows the two numbers. Over burden, nothing more can be placed. Alternative, prettier and more work:
    real DRAUGHT - the heavier she is the higher the sea stands against her side, a row at a time, until the
    lower deck's ports are under. I would build the first and keep the second for after it has been played.

Q6. CAN SHE BE LOST. Recommended: no. A hull block that is holed (a shark, a reef, a shot) becomes its "holed"
    twin and lets the sea in; water runs through the ship as the game's water does and spoils what stands in
    it until it is plugged and pumped. A ship that is awash is driven back to the calm sea she came from with
    her loose cargo overboard - and that cargo comes drifting past again over the next while. The keel, the
    hull and everything fixed down are never destroyed. Say if you want sinking to be final, or to be an option.

Q7. GETTING THINGS WITH NO GROUND TO DIG. This is the heart of it; section 2 has it at length. In short, six
    ways, each of which a bigger ship does better: what DRIFTS past, what is under the KEEL, what comes
    ALONGSIDE, what is GROWN aboard, what the ship's own WAY makes, and what OTHER SHIPS carry. Metal comes
    from wrecks first and from the sea bed after. Say if any of the six is not wanted.

Q8. FIGHTING. Recommended: none on the raft but a shark and a gull; from the caravel on, other ships - some to
    trade with, some that come for you. Guns are blocks. A gun fires at what is clicked on if it bears (it looks
    one way, and is turned with a wrench as Machinations' things are); it wants powder and shot from a magazine
    next to it. Boarders come over the rail and are fought on your own deck. Alternative: a peaceful sea -
    weather and beasts only - which costs the galleon and the dreadnought most of what they are for.

Q9. CREW. The rules allow three characters. Recommended: you begin alone; the second and third are CASTAWAYS,
    found adrift (the game's `becomes_player`). Beyond three, HANDS: hired or rescued, companions as the game
    has them, each of whom keeps one post - helm, pump, gun, galley, lookout - and does it while you do not.
    A dreadnought wants about twelve. No wages or mutiny (Plunder & Thunder planned those and they were cut).

Q10. AN END. Recommended: the sixth sea is the Black Water, and in the middle of it the MAELSTROM, and under that
    what made it. A dreadnought that goes in and comes out has finished the mod; the sea goes on after for as
    long as you care to build. Alternative: no end, only further seas.

Q11. DELIVERY. Recommended: five sittings, one a keel, each playable to its end -
    (1) the standalone switch, the sea, the raft, drift, hooks and nets, the first two seas;
    (2) the sailboat: masts and way, the helm and the chart, what comes alongside, growing things aboard;
    (3) the caravel: below the waterline, leaks and pumps, anchors, other ships, guns, castaways and hands;
    (4) the galleon: storms, cranes and salvage, the trawl and the bell, the foundry, the Ice;
    (5) the dreadnought: steel, steam, power, turrets, the Black Water and the Maelstrom.
    Say if you want it in one, and what to drop first. My order for dropping: the Ice, hands, real draught.

Q12. ART. Recommended: the game's own pictures for wood, cloth, water, glass, stone and the plain tools (it is
    the same art either way, and you said the mod uses the game's graphics), and fresh drawing, 16 by 16 doubled,
    for everything that is a ship's: sails, rigging, wheels, guns, plate, funnels, the creatures and the wrecks.
    Timber, bronze and steel fittings are to be different drawings, not one recoloured.

================================================================================================================
1. THE SHIP
================================================================================================================

Five keels. Length is along the water; "above" and "below" are decks' worth of height either side of it.

   KEEL          LONG  ABOVE  BELOW   WHAT IT IS                                  WHAT IT FIRST ALLOWS
   RAFT            10      3      0   lashed driftwood. No keel at all - the      a hook, a net, a rain barrel, a
                                      starting two planks and what is tied on     cooking fire on a slab of clay
   SAILBOAT        20     10      2   a real keel and one mast                    a helm and a chart: leaving the
                                                                                  first sea. A cabin. A plot of soil
   CARAVEL         40     18      5   two masts, a deck under water, a rail      an anchor, a hold, a pump, guns,
                                                                                  a ship's boat, a second character
   GALLEON         72     30      9   three masts, a gun deck, a great cabin,    a crane, a trawl, a diving bell,
                                      a forecastle and a poop                     a foundry on a stone hearth
   DREADNOUGHT    128     40     16   steel on frames. No masts. Boilers,         turrets, a dynamo, a searchlight,
                                      funnels, a bridge, a double bottom          a bathysphere, the last sea

A ship is whatever is joined to the keel. The shipwright's bench counts her up - as the game counts a rocket's
stack before a launch - and shows what she comes to:

   BURDEN     what the hull will bear, and what is aboard (Q5)
   WAY        how fast the sea goes by: sail set, or engine, against her weight. More way, more drift an hour
   FREEBOARD  how high her lowest open square stands above the water: what size of sea breaks over her
   STRENGTH   what the hull is made of: lashed wood, plank, tarred plank, copper-sheathed, ironbound, steel plate
   REACH      how far from her side things can be had: an arm's length, a hook, a boathook, a boat, a crane
   DEPTH      how far down she can fetch: a line, a pot, a trawl, a bell, a bathysphere

Those six numbers are the whole of how a bigger ship "paves the way": every sea asks for some of them and every
way of getting things is limited by one. Nothing is gated on having made a particular item.

PARTS, by kind (each a block or a few; about 380 in all, most in three or four materials that are drawn apart):
   HULL         planks, ribs, strakes, a stem and a stern piece, a rail, hatches, ports that open, ballast
   DECKS        boards, gratings (light and rain go through), ladders, companionways, a ship's lift for a dreadnought
   MASTS        mast sections, yards, tops, a crow's nest (see what is coming sooner), a bowsprit
   SAILS        squares and lateens in five cloths; SET or FURLED by a click - set in a gale, a sail tears
   STEERING     a steering oar, a tiller, a wheel, a binnacle; the helm is where a course is set (section 3)
   GROUND       a stone on a rope, a grapnel, a bower anchor and its capstan, a chain cable and steam windlass
   CATCHING     nets hung outboard, a fish trap, a lobster pot, a line, a trawl and its winch, a whaleboat's davits
   FETCHING     a hook, a boathook, a ship's boat, a derrick, a steam crane (lifts what nothing else will)
   UNDER        a glass-bottomed box, a diving helmet and its pump, a bell, a bathysphere on a thousand fathoms
   WATER        a rain barrel, a sailcloth catcher, a still, a cistern, a condenser - there is no fresh water
                but what is caught or boiled, and crops and crew both want it
   LIVING       hammocks, bunks, a galley stove, a table, a great cabin - the environment score as the game has it:
                a wet, cold deck wears a character down and a dry cabin with a stove mends them
   GROWING      planter boxes, tubs for trees, a glass frame, a hen coop, a goat pen, a mushroom locker in the bilge
   WORKING      the benches (section 4), all of which must stand on something that will take them: a fire on clay
                or stone, a foundry on a hearth of ballast, a boiler on iron
   KEEPING      crates, lockers, casks, a hold, a magazine (dry, or the powder is spoiled), an ice room
   FIGHTING     swivels, sakers, long guns, carronades, a mortar; then breech guns, turrets, a torpedo tube, plate
   POWER        a treadwheel, a paddle in the wake, a windmill; then a boiler, an engine, a shaft, a dynamo, lamps

================================================================================================================
2. WHERE THINGS COME FROM   (Q7)
================================================================================================================

1. DRIFT. The sea carries things past, on the surface and a little under it: driftwood, weed, bottles, casks,
   crates, spars with the canvas still on, a sea-chest, a hen coop with the hen in it. Each sea has its own
   (section 3). They come from ahead and are gone astern, so they must be had as they pass: by hand from the
   edge, with a HOOK thrown and hauled (a click on the thing), with a boathook, or without you - a NET hung
   outboard takes what touches it and holds it until it is emptied. More WAY, more of it. More length, more nets.
   A big ship also draws what a raft never sees: heavy flotsam rides low and wants a boat or a crane.

2. UNDER THE KEEL. Fish on a line, then in traps, then in a trawl dragged astern that brings up what lies on the
   bottom of the sea she is in - shell, sand, clay, weed, crab, and in the right seas nodules: the first ore
   that is not from a wreck. What comes up is by DEPTH. A diver goes down under the ship herself on the game's
   own breath bar: to scrape weed off the hull (it slows her), to plug a hole from outside, and in shoal water
   to walk the bed. A bell is a room of air let down on a cable; a bathysphere goes to the Black Water's floor.

3. ALONGSIDE. From the sailboat on, the lookout calls things the sea is bringing: a sandbar, a reef, an islet
   with three palms and a spring, a rock with seabirds, a wreck on a shoal, a derelict with nobody aboard, an
   iceberg, a dead whale, a floating market, a volcano's vent boiling the water. Such a thing is built of blocks
   in the open water beside the ship, and it is stripped: its trees felled, its soil dug and carried aboard, its
   stone quarried for ballast and hearths, its copper sheathing prised off, its guns swayed across by crane.
   A raft cannot stop, so it gets nothing of this. A sailboat can lie by for a little while. A ship with an
   ANCHOR holds a thing for as long as the cable and the weather allow - and the cable's length is which
   things: a stone on a rope holds a sandbar, a chain cable holds the edge of the deep. REACH is how you get
   across: a plank, a boat, a derrick. About seventy kinds, most of them in two or three sizes.
   THIS IS WHERE EARTH, STONE, SEEDS, BEASTS AND MOST METAL COME FROM. Nothing is mined; islands are eaten.

4. GROWN. Soil is cargo. Carried aboard and put in boxes it grows what was found: flax for canvas and rope, hemp,
   potatoes, limes (a crew with none sickens), cane, cotton, oak and pine in tubs for the timber no drift will
   give you in that size. Hens, goats, bees. Mushrooms in the dark of the bilge. All of it wants fresh water and
   room, and room is the keel.

5. THE SHIP'S OWN WAY. Things that work because she is moving or because she is tall: a paddle in the wake turns
   a shaft; a windmill turns another; a sailcloth catcher fills a cistern in rain; a salt pan on the deck dries
   brine in sun; a rod at the masthead in a thunderstorm charges a jar. Faster and taller is more of each. On a
   dreadnought the same shaft is turned by steam and the same jar is a dynamo.

6. OTHER SHIPS. Traders heave to and deal, at their own prices, in the mod's own coin (section 4). Derelicts are
   boarded for what is in them. Raiders bring their powder, guns and iron to you, and leave them.

WHAT METAL IS, STAGE BY STAGE
   raft         nails and hoops - burned out of driftwood and old casks. Enough for a hook and a knife
   sailboat     copper and lead - sheathing and scuppers off wrecks on the shoals; clay for a crucible
   caravel      iron - anchors, chain and guns out of derelicts; charcoal from your own tubs; a forge
   galleon      bronze and cast iron of your own - nodules from the trawl, coal from a collier's wreck or a seam
                in a sea-stack, sulphur from the vents, saltpetre from the bird rocks: your own powder
   dreadnought  steel - a converter that wants a hearth, a blast and power all at once; then plate, rivets, tube,
                wire, and at the last the black ore that only the bathysphere brings up

================================================================================================================
3. THE SEAS
================================================================================================================

Six seas. A course is set at the HELM, on a chart that shows the seas and what each asks of the ship; the helm
says in the ship's own six numbers what she lacks, and will not turn for a sea she cannot live in. Changing seas
takes a while of sailing, during which the backdrop, the weather and the drift turn from the one to the other.
(In the game a tier of portal is an age of progression. There are no portals here: the seas are the ages, and
the chart is where a new line of progression would be hung.)

   SEA              ASKS                          GIVES                                       AND BRINGS
   THE SLICK        nothing: you begin here       a wreck's leavings - wood, rope, casks,     gulls, a shark that
                                                  canvas, weed, small fish, nails             takes bites of a raft
   THE SHOALS       a keel, a helm, some way      sand, clay, stone, soil, palms, seeds,      reefs that hole
                                                  shellfish, copper, lead, turtles            plank; squalls
   THE TRADE WIND   an anchor, strength of        iron, cloth, tar, livestock, glass, charts, raiders; fever;
                    tarred plank, two masts       castaways, markets, derelicts               long calms
   THE ROARING SEA  freeboard for a gale,         coal, sulphur, saltpetre, whale oil, great  gales that tear set
                    sheathing, a pump             timber, bronze guns, sea-stacks, nodules    sail; whales; lightning
   THE ICE          ironbound bows, a stove       fresh water by the berg, ships frozen whole fog, cold, floes
                    in every cabin                with their stores, furs, the white bear     that nip a hull
   THE BLACK WATER  steel, steam, a searchlight   the black ore, the drowned fleet's plate    no day; things that
                                                  and engines, light-fish, the Maelstrom      come up; the Leviathan

Each sea has its own sky, water colour, swell, weather, drift table, fish, bed (what the trawl finds), things
that come alongside and ships. A day and a night pass as in the game; what drifts at night is not what drifts by
day. Going back to an easier sea is always allowed and is how a hurt ship mends.

WEATHER MATTERS BECAUSE THE SHIP IS BLOCKS. A sea has a height. In a blow it rises, and any square below it that
is open to the outside takes water - which runs down hatches and along decks exactly as the game's water does,
puts out fires, spoils powder and food it reaches and has to be pumped. So freeboard, hatches that shut, ports
that close and a tight hull are not ornaments. Sail left set in a gale tears; a ship lying to an anchor in one
drags and loses what she was holding.

BEASTS (about 60, every one cageable and with a body): gull, booby, albatross (ill luck to kill), flying fish,
dolphin (leads you to drift), shark, swordfish (holes plank), turtle, seal, walrus, white bear, whale and its
calf, giant squid, the things on the reefs, the things in the weed, a rat that comes aboard with a cargo and
eats the stores, a ship's cat that deals with it, the drowned crews of the Black Water, and the Leviathan -
which is built big, in many parts, not drawn large.

================================================================================================================
4. MAKING, KEEPING AND TRADING
================================================================================================================

BENCHES. The first is the SHIPWRIGHT'S BENCH, and as in the game it makes benches, machines and the things to
trade at and nothing else - and lays keels, which is its upgrade. It has a level for each keel. The rest, each
taking what suits it: a ropewalk, a sail loft, a sawpit, a caulker's kettle, a cooper's block, a galley, a
smokehouse, a chandler's table, a kiln, a forge, a gunfounder's pit, a powder mill, a chart table, a
sickbay, then an engine shop, a plate mill, an electrical bench and an armoury. About 22.

LONG MAKINGS. A sail is flax grown, retted, spun, woven, cut, sewn, roped and bent to a yard: eight. A galleon's
keel is ten deep and a dreadnought's fifteen, and the generator will refuse to write the mod if a shorter way
to either turns up.

TRADE. Trading ships and floating markets, in the mod's own three coins (they read as coins do in the game: a
hundred of one is one of the next). Every item has a category and a price, as the loader demands; the categories
are the mod's own (timber, cordage and canvas, metal, fittings, guns, stores, livestock, curios, benches,
creatures). What a trader will deal in rises with the sea she is met in.

A QUEST BOOK of its own, written by `tools/quests.py` from the data as every other is: a tab a keel, each a tree.

================================================================================================================
5. WHAT THE ENGINE MUST LEARN   (all of it as data fields the base game or another mod could use)
================================================================================================================

 1. `"standalone": true` on a mod (`src/mods.c`): its directory is loaded in place of `data/`; nothing else may
    be on with it. The engine already knows no content by name, and the tests already run it against a second
    small set of data (`tests/legacy`) - but the base game has never been ABSENT, and something will turn out
    to lean on it. Finding those is the first job, and each is a bug in the engine by its own rules.
 2. A world generator `"adrift"`: sea from a waterline down, no bed, and a start structure named in the data.
    A small world of its own size, set in `worldgen.json`.
 3. A moving backdrop (`"drift"` in the rules; drawn in `src/main.c` behind the world pass): layers with speeds,
    the swell, the wake, all by the ship's way. And the sea's surface drawn rising and falling with the weather.
 4. Things adrift: the game's drops, given a table to appear from (`"drift"` on a sea), a speed along the
    surface, and an end when they leave the world. `"catches"` on a block (a net); `"hook": {reach}` on an item.
 5. The ship counted (`game_ship_report`, in the manner of `game_rocket_report`): everything joined to a block
    with `"keel": {long, above, below, level}`; `"ship": {burden, weight, sail, thrust, strength}` on blocks.
    Placing outside the envelope, or over burden, is refused.
 6. Seas: `"seas"` in `worldgen.json` (what each asks, gives and brings); a block with `"helm"` and its screen
    (a new `src/helmui.c`). Which sea, and how far along a passage, is saved BY NAME: a new save version, with a
    fixture of the one before, and nothing else in the format changes.
 7. Coming alongside: `"alongside"` on a sea - structures (the system villages and wrecks use) set down in the
    open water beside the ship and taken away again. What a player has put there or left there is brought back
    aboard as drops, never deleted. `"anchor": {cable}` on a block decides what can be held and how long.
 8. Holes: `"holed"` on a hull block names its twin, which the sea comes through (a state that must survive a
    save is a block of its own, as with levers); `"mends"` on an item puts it back. `"pump"` on a block.
    A square `"spoils"` what is stored in it when wet, if the data says so.
 9. `"trawl": {depth}` and `"bell"`: machines that bring up from the sea's bed table, by way and by depth - the
    same shape as the miner Industry already has.
10. Guns as blocks: `"gun": {power, range, takes}`, fired by the click at what it bears on, turned by an item
    that `"turns"`. Other ships: Plunder & Thunder's `"ship"` creature and `"boarding"`, which are in the
    engine already and only need the boarders put on MY deck instead of theirs.
11. `"needs_under"` on a block: what it must stand on (a forge on stone). One placement rule.
12. Tests: `test_hull_mod` loads the mod ALONE and checks its counts, the keels, a ship counted, a sea refused
    and allowed, a net filled, a thing brought alongside and taken away with the player's blocks kept, a hole
    made and mended, and an old save loaded. Every existing test must pass untouched: with the mod off, not one
    random number is drawn that was not drawn before and no world changes.

================================================================================================================
6. WHAT I AM NOT SURE OF
================================================================================================================

   - Whether the first hour is good. A raft game lives or dies on whether hooking things out of the sea is a
     pleasure, and that is feel, which cannot be tested here and will not have been played.
   - How much of the base game the engine quietly expects. Item 1 above may be an afternoon or a week.
   - The Leviathan and the Maelstrom are the least worked-out part of this plan and the likeliest to come out
     smaller than they read.
   - Seventy things to come alongside, each drawn and built by hand in several sizes, is the largest single
     piece of work in it and the first place it would be cut.
   - Balance, prices, how often anything turns up: guesses, as in every mod so far.

WHAT WAS DECIDED, WHAT WAS BUILT, AND WHAT WAS NOT   (October 2026)
================================================================================================================

THE OWNER'S ANSWER: "You can decide anything yourself. Make the mod. NO ending." So every recommendation above
was taken, except Q10: there is no Maelstrom and nothing that finishes the mod. The Leviathan is a creature of
the last sea and no more.

BUILT (tools/mod_hull_and_horizon.py; engine in src/ship.c and src/helmui.c; tests in test_hull_mod)
   - Q1: `"standalone": true` in a mod.json. Such a mod is loaded in place of data/, alone.
   - Q2: the "adrift" world: 768 by 160 of sea, the start raft, a moving sky, foam, far rocks; things adrift that
     really move, a square at a time, and go under the ship.
   - Q3/Q4: five keels as levels of the shipwright's bench - raft, sailboat, caravel, galleon, dreadnought -
     each a room to build in. Makings 4, 7, 12 and 16 deep, asserted by the generator.
   - Q5: burden, the simple way. Also way, freeboard, strength, cable, reach, depth, guns: counted from blocks.
   - Q7: drift (hooks, nets); the bed (pot, trawl, bell, bathysphere); 35 structures that come alongside and are
     stripped; crops in boxes and trees in tubs; trade desks and markets. Metal as planned: scrap, then copper,
     iron, bronze, steel, black alloy.
   - Six seas with needs, a helm with a menu, passages, save version 42.
   - Q9, in part: castaways become characters at the ship's bell.
   - Q12: every picture drawn fresh by the generator, 16 by 16 doubled; the seven hulls each drawn their own way.
   - A book of quests: five tabs, a tab a keel, each a tree. Bilge pumps. Engines that want fuel. Sails that furl.
   SIZE, AS BUILT: 205 blocks, 463 items (37 caged creatures), 314 recipes, 37 creatures, 18 kinds of bench,
   35 structures, 106 quests - against ~380 / ~850 / ~1,000 / ~60 / ~70 proposed. About half.

NOT BUILT
   - Q6: holes, leaks, anything that hurts the hull, being driven back to a calmer sea. Nothing damages the ship.
   - Weather at sea: no swell that rises, no water over a low deck, no sail torn in a gale. Freeboard is only
     something a sea asks for.
   - Q5's second way: real draught.
   - Q8 as written: guns are not aimed and want no powder - they fire by themselves, all as one. No other ships
     under way and no boarding: a raider is a hull that comes alongside with raiders on its deck.
   - Q9: hands with posts. Q10: the Maelstrom (by the owner's word).
   - Section 2's fifth way (paddles in the wake, windmills, lightning rods, salt pans): none of it. Power is not
     a thing: a dynamo is furniture.
   - Fresh water as a need of crew or crops; a fire needing clay or stone under it (`"needs_under"`).
   - Diving as a way of getting things: one may swim down, but there is nothing there but what is alongside.
   - The Ice and the Black Water have five things alongside each, the others seven or eight.
   - NEVER PLAYED. Looked at in screenshots: the raft by day and night, a caravel in the Trade Wind with the
     helm's menu open, a steel hull in the Black Water. Drift rates, what each sea asks, burden and weights,
     prices and creatures' strength are all guesses. The progression was walked on paper once for dead ends
     (three were found and taken out); it has not been walked in the game.

AFTERWARDS (the owner: "Continue and finish.")
   BUILT SINCE
   - Q6, in part: what hunts bites through hull too weak for the sea she is in (`"holed"` on a block: its twin,
     which is not hull and lets the sea in). Stronger hull is the answer, and pumps.
