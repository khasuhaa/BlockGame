PROPOSAL 4 - HEARTH AND HOLLOW   (a settlers mod; medium to large; for approval - nothing is built)

Everything in this world is done by the one who is played. Industry automates with machines and Machinations with
pistons; Rags to Riches has villagers, but they stand in their houses and sell. This mod is the third way to get
things done: PEOPLE. Settlers who come because you built somewhere worth coming to, who have a trade, a home, a
temper and a day, and who do the work of a settlement while you do something else.

It is not a machine with a face on it. A settler who is cold, hungry, crowded or bored works badly and at last
leaves; one who is well housed and well fed gets better at their trade and brings others. The thing you build is
not a factory but a place people want to be.

Size: ~150 blocks, ~300 items, ~350 recipes, 16 trades, and a great deal of behaviour.

================================================================================================================
0. QUESTIONS
================================================================================================================

Q1. HOW MUCH THEY ARE TOLD. Two schools:
      A. You ZONE and they decide: mark a field, a quarry face, a stockpile, a workshop; settlers of the right
         trade find the work themselves. Recommended: it is the less tiring to play and the more alive to watch.
      B. You give each settler orders. More control, and a second job for the player.
    Recommended: A, with one override - any settler can be told "do this now".

Q2. HOW THEY ARRIVE. Recommended: by what you have built. A bed with a roof and a table brings a wanderer. Each
    kind of settler wants something to be there first (a smith comes to a forge; a brewer to a hop field and a
    cellar). A notice board says who is thinking of coming and what would decide them.

Q3. CAN THEY DIE. Recommended: they can be hurt, fall ill, and leave; they do not die. Losing a named settler to
    a dropbear would be a cruelty the rest of the game does not have.

Q4. HOW MANY. Recommended: up to about 40 in a world. Each one finds its way about, which is the costliest
    thing the game does; forty is a number the game can afford, and a village's worth.

Q5. WITH RAGS TO RICHES. Its villages stay as they are. Where both are on, a village's people can be invited to
    your settlement, and yours can carry goods to market there. Neither needs the other.

Q6. DELIVERY. Recommended: three stages - (1) settlers, homes, needs, hauling, farming, the notice board;
    (2) the trades and workshops; (3) festivals, visitors, the council and the town's ranks.

================================================================================================================
1. A SETTLER
================================================================================================================

Each has a name, a face (the character model, in their own clothes), a TRADE, a skill in it that rises with
practice (apprentice, journeyman, master), and two or three TRAITS drawn from forty: early riser, glutton,
green-fingered, afraid of the dark, sings at work (those near work faster), light sleeper, hates rain, tidy,
gossip, loner, brave. Traits are why one cook is not another.

A DAY. They wake, eat, go to work, stop to eat, work, come home, spend the evening somewhere (the hearth, the
inn, the square), and sleep. Seasons and weather change the day: nobody ploughs in a storm. Watching this go
round is half the pleasure of the mod.

NEEDS, each a bar: food (and variety - the same stew every day palls), rest (a bed of their own, quiet),
warmth, shelter, company, beauty (flowers, lamps, a view - the game's environment score, applied to them),
leisure, and purpose (work that suits them, and enough of it). The bars make a MOOD, and mood sets how well
and how fast they work, whether they stay, and whether they speak well of the place to others.

HOMES. A bed claims a room; a room is judged by its size, light, floor, what is in it and who else is. A
family wants a house. Better homes are the long project: a settlement that began as huts ends as a town.

================================================================================================================
2. TRADES
================================================================================================================

Sixteen, each with a workplace that must be built, tools that must be supplied, and three ranks of skill:

   FARMER       sows, waters, reaps and replants zoned fields; at master, rotates crops and saves the best seed
   HERDER       feeds, shears, milks and breeds whatever is penned
   WOODCUTTER   fells and replants a zoned wood; never the tree you marked to keep
   MINER        digs a face you have marked, props the roof, brings ore out - never deeper than is lit
   HAULER       carries from where things are made to where they are wanted; the settlement's blood
   BUILDER      builds what you lay out as a GHOST: place blocks as outlines and they fetch and set them
   COOK         cooks what the larder allows into meals; variety is their craft
   SMITH, CARPENTER, MASON, TAILOR    each keeps a workshop making what its ORDER BOARD lists, from stock
   BREWER       beer, cider, mead: the inn's trade, and a festival's
   HEALER       tends the hurt and the ill; grows herbs
   GUARD        walks a round by night; sees off what comes
   TEACHER      an apprentice near a teacher learns twice as fast; children (Q below) grow up skilled
   MERCHANT     takes the surplus to a trade portal or a village and comes back with what was asked for

The BUILDER is the trade that changes the game most: a wall, a road, a house laid out as ghost blocks and
built while you are away. The HAULER is the one that makes the rest work.

ORDER BOARDS. A workshop makes what its board says: "keep 20 bread", "make 5 iron picks", "until told to stop".
STOCKPILES say what may be kept where. Between them, a settlement runs itself or jams, and unjamming it is
the puzzle.

================================================================================================================
3. THE SETTLEMENT
================================================================================================================

It has a name, a banner, and a RANK that rises with its people and what it has: camp, hamlet, village, town,
free city. Each rank opens buildings and brings a kind of visitor.

BUILDINGS that are more than rooms because of what happens in them: the hearth (company, at first), a well, a
larder, a granary, an inn (leisure; travellers; news), a bathhouse, a school, a shrine, an infirmary, a market
square, a watchtower, a town hall with a council table.

VISITORS: pedlars, a tinker who mends, travelling players (a night's leisure for everyone), a tax-farmer you
may refuse, pilgrims, a rival settlement's envoy, and wanderers who may stay.

FESTIVALS, four a year by the season: sowing, midsummer, harvest, midwinter. Each wants things laid in (food,
drink, lanterns, a bonfire, music), lasts a day and a night, and leaves the whole place in good heart for a
season - or, skimped, the reverse.

THE COUNCIL. At village rank, settlers ask for things: a bridge, a bigger inn, fewer hours in winter, the
dropbears in the east wood dealt with. Granting them is the mod's quests, and they come from what is actually
wrong with your settlement, not from a list.

CHILDREN, optionally: families in good homes have them; they play, go to school, and grow into settlers with
a parent's trait and a trade of their choosing. A settlement that can replace itself.

================================================================================================================
4. WHAT IT MAKES
================================================================================================================

Tools of each trade in the game's metals; work clothes (an apron, a miner's cap with a lamp, a herder's crook);
furniture that a room is judged by (tables, chairs, rugs, shelves, curtains, a mantel, a cradle, a writing
desk - about 80 pieces, in four woods and three styles); signs and boards; carts and barrows that haulers use;
the buildings' special blocks; festival goods (lanterns, bunting, a maypole, a long table, fireworks); a
LEDGER that shows everything the settlement has, makes, eats and lacks.

================================================================================================================
5. ENGINE
================================================================================================================

New, as data fields:
   - people: creatures with the character's body, an inventory, needs, a schedule, and a list of jobs they can
     take - found by looking for work within reach, done with the same actions a character has;
   - ZONES: rectangles of the world with a purpose (field, wood, quarry, stockpile, pen, no-go);
   - GHOST BLOCKS: a layer of blocks that are wanted, not there yet;
   - rooms: a flood-fill that knows a room's size and contents (a space mod would use the same);
   - order boards and stock rules as data kept with a block;
   - route-finding for many walkers cheaply (shared paths along roads, which is also why roads are worth building);
   - moods, traits, and a notice board that explains itself.
Reused: the character model and its poses, actions and tools (they do what you do), creatures' saving,
structures, the environment score, beds, seasons and weather, signals (a bell that calls everyone in).

HARD PARTS: forty walkers on a big world without slowing it (Q4) - the engine's route-finding is sized for one
character and would need to be bounded and shared; settlers that never get stuck, never starve beside a full
larder, and never carry the last bread away from the cook; saying WHY a settler is unhappy plainly enough that
it is a puzzle and not a mystery.
