PLUNDER & THUNDER - PROPOSAL   (a pirate mod; MAJOR; for approval - nothing below is built)

Turn the mod on and a new world is not land with a sea at its edges. It is SEA, with land in it: hundreds of
small islands, each its own little country, a day's sail apart. On some of them, towns. Between them, ships -
merchants to rob or to guard, the navy to run from, other pirates to fight. Under them, wrecks. And on a great
many beaches, an X.

You begin shipwrecked on a sandbar with a plank and a bottle. You end as the terror of the sea, or its admiral,
or the richest merchant in it, with a fleet, a fort and a flag of your own design.

    a raft  ->  a jolly boat  ->  a sloop and a crew of three  ->  a brig, a letter of marque or a black flag  ->
    a frigate and a harbour of your own  ->  a man-of-war, a fleet, and the Kraken's hoard

(Proposal1, "Tides of Fortune", is also about the sea. That one is a sailing-and-diving mod laid over the game's
own worlds. This one REPLACES the world, and is about people: towns, crews, factions, robbery and reputation.
They share an engine - boats - and little else. If both are wanted, this should be built first.)

Size aimed at: ~450 blocks, ~900 items, ~1,100 recipes, ~50 creatures, 9 ships, ~70 kinds of island and building.

================================================================================================================
0. QUESTIONS
================================================================================================================

Q1. THE WORLD. Recommended: with the mod on, every new world is an OCEAN WORLD - about nine parts sea in ten,
    islands 12 to 120 blocks long scattered by the seed, a few island CHAINS, one or two big islands with
    mountains on them, and the game's own ores and caves under each. A world option says how many islands and how
    far apart. Worlds made before the mod are left alone (and get nothing - a world cannot be re-made); say if you
    would rather they got a "Far Isles" place to sail to, as Dimensions makes its places.

Q2. WHAT A SHIP IS. As in Proposal1: recommended, a VEHICLE - built at a shipyard, launched, sailed with the
    movement keys, with a hold, a hull that takes damage, and FITTINGS in slots (sails, guns, a figurehead). Not a
    thing made of blocks that moves: the engine cannot move a piece of the world. But the ship's DECK is somewhere
    you can stand and walk and fight - without that there is no boarding, and a pirate mod without boarding is
    not one.

Q3. HOW FIGHTING AT SEA WORKS. Recommended, and it must be played by clicks as the rest of the game is:
      - GUNS fire broadsides at what is clicked on: slow to reload, a range, three kinds of shot (round for the
        hull, chain for the sails - a ship with no sails cannot run - grape for the crew);
      - a ship has HULL, SAILS and CREW, each a bar; lose the hull and she sinks (and leaves a wreck to dive);
        lose the sails and she is caught; lose the crew and she strikes her colours;
      - close enough, GRAPPLE and BOARD: the two decks are joined and it is a fight on foot, yours against theirs;
      - a ship that strikes can be PLUNDERED (her hold), RANSOMED (coin, and she remembers you kindly), SUNK, or
        TAKEN AS A PRIZE - sailed home by some of your crew and added to your fleet or sold.
    Say if you would rather sea fights were quicker and simpler: guns only, no boarding.

Q4. A CREW. The game allows three characters. A brig wants twenty hands. Recommended: your characters are the
    OFFICERS; the CREW are hired in taverns and are not characters - they are a number on the ship with a morale,
    a wage and a share of the plunder, plus a few NAMED hands with a skill each (gunner, bosun, carpenter,
    surgeon, cook, navigator, lookout, quartermaster) who stand at their posts and can be seen. Unpaid, unfed or
    beaten too often, they mutiny.

Q5. WHOSE SIDE. Recommended: four POWERS on the sea - the Crown (navy; law; forts), the Company (merchants;
    money), the Brethren (pirates; free ports), the Islanders (the people who were here first; they hold the old
    maps) - and a standing with each, from Hunted to Honoured, moved by everything you do. It decides which
    harbours let you in, what they charge, who attacks on sight, and which of the three long roads below you are
    on. Alternative: no powers; everyone is neutral until fired on.

Q6. BURIED TREASURE. Recommended: real digging. A map is a drawing of an island's outline with a mark; you must
    find the island that matches (the game shows you the outline of each island you have seen), land, pace it
    out from the landmark the map names, and dig. No arrow, no glowing spot. Riddles and half-maps for the big
    ones. Alternative: a map simply shows where.

Q7. RUM, AND TASTE. Pirates drink, fight, rob and are hanged. Recommended tone: storybook - rum is a drink with
    an effect, nobody is tortured, surrender is always possible, a hanging is a wanted poster and a gaol you can
    be broken out of. Say if you want it rougher.

Q8. WITH OTHER MODS. It depends on none. With Rags to Riches on, its villages do not also appear (this mod's
    towns are the towns); its trade rules apply in them. With Machinations, ships' guns can be loaded by its
    machinery. Seeker of the Dao and Beyond the Sky are simply further things to do from an island.

Q9. DELIVERY. This is not one sitting unless it is cut hard. Recommended: five stages, each playable -
    (1) the ocean world and small boats; (2) towns, trade, taverns, the four powers; (3) big ships, guns, crews,
    sea fights and boarding; (4) treasure, maps, wrecks, the islands' secrets; (5) forts, fleets, your own
    harbour, the three endgames. Say if you want it in one, and what to drop.

================================================================================================================
1. THE WORLD
================================================================================================================

ISLANDS, by kind - each is a small biome with its own ground, trees and reason to land (about 30 kinds):
   sandbar            nothing but sand and a palm: where you begin
   cay                palms, turtles, coconuts, fresh water if you dig
   jungle isle        thick, tall, loud; fruit, hardwood, parrots, things in the undergrowth
   volcano            a cone with a crater lake of lava; obsidian, sulphur, hot springs; it rumbles
   atoll              a ring of reef round a lagoon; pearls; one way in, and that at high tide
   mangrove           trees standing in the sea; a maze for small boats; crabs; a smugglers' landing
   cliff isle         sheer on every side; seabirds; a cave at the waterline that goes right through
   skull rock         it looks like one. The eyes are caves.
   plantation isle    cane, tobacco, cotton, a great house; the Company's
   fortress rock      the Crown's: walls, a battery, a gaol, a governor
   free port          the Brethren's: no law, every flag, the best tavern on the sea
   stilt village      the Islanders': houses over the water, canoes, a carved pole, a keeper of maps
   whaling station, fishing hamlet, lighthouse rock, leper isle, hermit's isle, marooner's rock,
   shipwreck shoal, the sargasso (weed so thick a ship sticks), a fog bank that never lifts, a graveyard of
   ships, an isle that is the back of something, an isle that is only there at low tide, and THE ISLE THAT
   IS NOT ON ANY CHART.

THE SEA: shallows you can run aground in, reefs that hole a hull, deep channels, currents (the trade routes),
a wind that shifts through the day and blows steady in the trades. Tides, two a day: they open sea caves, float
grounded ships, and cover the low isles. Weather: squalls, gales, calms (a becalmed crew drinks the water), fog,
waterspouts, and once or twice a year a HURRICANE that changes the beaches.

UNDER IT ALL the game's own ground, ores and caves - reached from an island's own mine, or a sea cave.

================================================================================================================
2. SHIPS
================================================================================================================

   RAFT          logs and rope. Paddled. Sinks in a squall. The first hour.
   JOLLY BOAT    four oars and a little sail: goes up creeks; every big ship carries one
   CANOE         the Islanders': fast, narrow, two aboard, no hold - and rides a reef that would sink a sloop
   SLOOP         one mast, 4 guns, hold of 16, crew of 6. The first real ship. Fast; runs from anything
   SCHOONER      two masts fore-and-aft, 8 guns: the smuggler's and the pirate's favourite
   BRIG          two masts, 14 guns, hold of 48, crew of 20
   MERCHANTMAN   fat, slow, hold of 120, 8 guns: the prize, and the trader's ship
   FRIGATE       three masts, 28 guns, crew of 50: the navy's hunter
   MAN-OF-WAR    two gun decks, 60 guns: a floating fort. There are three on the sea, and you may come to own one

EVERY SHIP is yours to fit out, in slots: SAILS (canvas, patched, silk, black - black is seen from less far),
GUNS (swivels, sakers, long nines, carronades, a mortar that fires over walls), the HULL (plank, tarred,
copper-sheathed, ironbound), a FIGUREHEAD (each a small boon: the mermaid calms the crew, the lion frightens a
merchant into striking sooner, the saint mends in a storm), a RAM, boarding nets, a crow's nest (see further), a
ship's bell, a pump, a galley, a surgeon's table, a brig for prisoners, a captain's cabin with a chest and a bed
- and a FLAG, which you design: a field, a device from thirty, a colour. Ships that have heard of you strike
when they see it.

A ship wears: weed on the hull slows her until she is CAREENED on a beach; shot holes leak until a carpenter
plugs them; sails tear. A ship has a NAME (you give it) and a reputation of her own.

YOUR FLEET. Prizes can be kept: up to five ships, each with a captain from among your named hands, following
the flagship, or sent on errands - to carry a cargo, to guard a harbour, to cruise for prizes on their own and
come back with what they took, or not come back.

================================================================================================================
3. TOWNS, TRADE AND THE FOUR POWERS
================================================================================================================

About forty towns in a world, of six kinds, each built from buildings drawn by hand (the structure system):
   HARBOUR      quay, warehouses, a market, a chandler, a shipyard, a customs house, a church, a tavern
   FORT TOWN    all that under the guns of a fort: the governor, the gaol, the gallows, the naval yard
   PLANTATION   the great house, the mill, the barracoons turned to stores, a private jetty
   FREE PORT    a town of taverns: fences who buy anything, a surgeon who asks nothing, a hall where the
                captains vote
   STILT TOWN   the Islanders': a longhouse, the map-keeper, the canoe sheds, a shrine on a rock
   OUTPOST      a jetty, a store, a signal fire

WHAT A TOWN DOES. It makes some things and wants others, and its prices say so (the game's trade engine, with
each town its own prices as Rags to Riches' villages have): sugar is cheap where cane grows and dear where it
does not, powder is dear everywhere the navy is not. CARGO in bulk - sugar, rum, tobacco, cotton, timber, salt
fish, spice, cloth, tools, powder, silver - is the merchant's game. Prices move when you flood a market, and
with war, storms and blockade.
   THE TAVERN   hire hands; hear RUMOURS (a merchantman left Port Royal two days ago, low in the water; a man
                in the corner has half a map; the navy's frigate is careening at Turtle Cay) - each a real thing
                in the world for a while; gamble (liar's dice, a real game); buy a round for morale
   THE SHIPYARD build, repair, fit out, careen, rename
   THE GOVERNOR letters of marque, bounties, pardons (dear), a daughter or son with opinions
   THE FENCE    sells what cannot be sold elsewhere, at a price
   THE CHURCH   a pirate may bury a friend, or give to the poor (it is noticed)
   THE GAOL     where you wake if taken. Bribe, wait, be broken out by your crew, or escape through the drain

THE POWERS (Q5) hold towns, send ships, and are at war or peace with one another, which changes through a game.
A town can CHANGE HANDS: sack it for the Brethren, take it for the Crown, buy it for the Company - and the sea
is a different sea afterwards. Your standing with each is on a page of its own.

THREE LONG ROADS, and they can be mixed:
   THE BLACK FLAG   Rob merchants, sack towns, grow a legend. NOTORIETY is a number the whole sea knows: it
                    makes merchants strike sooner, hands cheaper to hire, and the navy send more and better
                    ships. At the end: the pirate round-table, and the title King (or Queen) of the Brethren.
   THE KING'S COMMISSION  Hunt pirates, escort convoys, take enemy forts. Rank: lieutenant to admiral. At the
                    end: the flagship, a governorship, and the Brethren's last stronghold to break.
   THE COMPANY LEDGER  Buy cheap, sell dear, own warehouses, then plantations, then the shipyards, then the
                    governors. At the end: a monopoly, and the choice of what to do with it.
   And under all three, the Islanders' road: the old maps, and what they lead to (5).

================================================================================================================
4. FIGHTING, CREW AND A PIRATE'S LIFE
================================================================================================================

AT SEA, as Q3. Wind matters: the ship upwind chooses the range. A lookout calls sails before you see them; a
spyglass tells you what she is, what she flies, how low she rides. False colours are allowed, and remembered.

ON DECK, boarding: cutlass, boarding axe, pistols (one shot, slow to load - carry a brace), blunderbuss, pike,
grenadoes, a belaying pin. Cut the halyards and a sail comes down on them. Fight the captain and the rest give
in. Your named hands fight beside you.

ASHORE: sack a town - land, take the battery from behind, hold the square till the governor pays. Or do it
quietly: over the wall at night for the strongroom.

THE CREW (Q4). Articles are SIGNED at the start of a cruise: shares, rules, what a lost arm is worth. Morale
rises with prizes, rum, shore leave, fair shares and a captain who fights; falls with short rations, long
calms, lost fights and a hoarding captain. Low: desertion in port. Lower: a black spot, and a vote.
   Named hands grow in their trades, have a home island, a grudge, a thing they want. Some are found, not
   hired: a marooned navigator, a gunner in the gaol at Fort Royal, a cook who is more than he says.

SICKNESS AND STORES. Water and food for the crew by the day (a hold of plunder is no use to a starving ship);
scurvy without fruit; rum is water that keeps. Stores are what decides how far from port you dare go.

BEING WANTED. A price on your head, shown on posters in every Crown town with a drawing that gets more like you
as it rises. Bounty hunters. At the top, the three men-of-war are sent, together.

================================================================================================================
5. TREASURE, AND WHAT IS OUT THERE
================================================================================================================

BURIED TREASURE (Q6). Maps come from rumours, from prizes' cabins, from the fence, from a dying man. Kinds:
   a plain map      an island's shape and an X
   a paced map      "from the split rock, forty paces to the dead palm, then dig in its noon shadow"
   a torn map       in two, three or four pieces, held by people who do not like each other
   a riddle         no drawing at all
   a false map      someone wanted you on that beach
A chest is dug for with a shovel, through sand and whatever was laid over it. Some are trapped. Some are
guarded - by crabs, by the dead who buried them, by the other crew who had the other half of the map.
And you may BURY YOUR OWN: what is in the ground cannot be taken with your ship, and the game draws you a map.

WRECKS, on the sea floor: dive with a breath, a bell, a diving dress. Every ship sunk in the world - by you or
by anyone - becomes one, with whatever she carried. The Plate Fleet went down in a hurricane a lifetime ago,
eleven ships, and nobody has found the eleventh.

THE ISLANDS' SECRETS, a long thread for those who look:
   - carved stones on twelve islands that, read together (the Islanders teach the reading), give bearings;
   - a sea cave that is a harbour, with a ship in it that is not rotten;
   - a volcano's vault that opens when the lava is low;
   - the Isle That Is Not On Any Chart, found only by sailing where the bearings cross, in fog;
   - the GHOST SHIP, which hunts the most notorious captain on the sea, cannot be sunk by shot, and can be laid
     to rest;
   - the KRAKEN, in the deep channel, which has taken a great many ships and kept what was in them;
   - and the Islanders' own treasure, which is not gold, and which they will show to someone they honour.

CREATURES (about 50): parrots and monkeys (both tameable; a monkey can be sent aloft or through a window),
turtles, crabs of five sizes, boars, snakes, caimans in the mangroves, sharks, rays, barracuda, a giant
octopus, sea serpents, the drowned crews of wrecks, a white whale. Ship's cats. A dog that finds buried things.

================================================================================================================
6. WHAT IT MAKES
================================================================================================================

Materials: ship's timbers in three woods, pitch, tar, oakum, canvas, cordage, copper sheet, powder (charcoal,
sulphur from volcanoes, saltpetre from cave floors), shot in three kinds, cane and sugar, molasses, RUM in six
ages (a cask in a cellar gets better by the year), tobacco, cotton, indigo, spice, salt, salt pork, hardtack,
limes, pearls, coral, ambergris, tortoiseshell, pieces of eight, doubloons, emeralds, idols.
Benches: shipyard (5 grades), sawpit, ropewalk, sail loft, powder mill, gunfounder, cooperage, still and
cellar, sugar mill, smokehouse, chart table, the captain's desk (articles, the log, the flag).
Building, for a harbour of your own: quays, pilings, piers, cranes, warehouses, a careening beach, sea walls,
palisades, bastions, gun batteries that fire on what you are at war with, a signal tower, a lighthouse, a
tavern that draws hands, a governor's house if you mean to be one.
Clothes that are a statement: tricorn, bandana, eyepatch (see in the dark below decks), a coat in the colour
of your power, sea boots, a sash for the pistols, a wooden leg (it happens) - with sets.
LONG MAKINGS, as the owner likes: a sloop is about eight makings deep (felled timber -> seasoned -> sawn ->
frames -> keel -> hull -> masted -> rigged), a frigate about fourteen, a man-of-war cannot be made at all
until a shipyard of the fifth grade stands in a harbour you own - about twenty.

================================================================================================================
7. ENGINE
================================================================================================================

New, as data fields any mod could use:
   - a WORLD generator for sea with islands in it (the home world's generator is fixed today: this is the
     biggest single piece, and it must leave worlds made without the mod exactly as they were);
   - vehicles on water with a walkable deck, wind, hull/sails/crew, fittings, guns that fire at a target;
   - vehicles that are not yours: ships with a purpose (a route, a patrol, a hunt), that fight, flee and strike;
   - two decks joined for a fight; a number of fighters each side who are not creatures one by one;
   - towns with an owner, stock, prices that move, and events (the rumours);
   - factions and standing; notoriety; a bounty;
   - crew as a quantity with morale, and named hands with posts;
   - maps as items that carry a picture and a place, and island outlines the player has seen;
   - tides and currents (as levels and fields, not as simulated water);
   - things buried: an item put in the ground and remembered; casks that age.
Reused: vehicles and riding (trains), structures and villages (towns), trade and per-place prices (Rags to
Riches), effects (rum, scurvy), relic slots (figureheads), taming and companions (parrot, monkey, dog), sized
creatures and swimming, strikes from afar (guns), formulas (charts and plans), sets, the mission board idea
from Seeker of the Dao (bounty boards), signs (wanted posters).

HARD PARTS, said now:
   - the ocean world generator, and performance on a world that is mostly water;
   - a deck you can walk on while it moves, and two of them locked together;
   - ships with minds: forty towns and perhaps sixty ships going about their business without slowing the game
     (they should be moved coarsely when far away and properly only when near);
   - a sea fight that is fun by clicks (Q3) - prototype this before anything else is promised;
   - treasure maps that can be solved by looking (Q6): the island outline picture is new interface;
   - tone (Q7), and three endgames that are each worth reaching.
