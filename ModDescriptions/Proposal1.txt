PROPOSAL 1 - TIDES OF FORTUNE   (a seafaring mod; MAJOR, on the scale of Dimensions; for approval - nothing is built)

The game has an ocean and almost nothing to do with it: a biome at the edge of the land with fish, sharks, kelp and
coral in it. This mod makes the sea a second world as big as the land - one you cross in a ship you built, dive
under in a suit you made, fish, chart, salvage and fight on. No other mod touches water as a place.

    a raft and a pole  ->  a sloop  ->  a brig with a hold and guns  ->  a diving bell  ->  a submarine  ->
    the trench, and what lives at the bottom of it

It is meant to add about as much as Dimensions did: ~350 blocks, ~700 items, ~800 recipes, ~55 creatures.

================================================================================================================
0. QUESTIONS
================================================================================================================

Q1. WHAT A SHIP IS. Two ways, and they are very different amounts of work:
      A. A ship is a VEHICLE, as the train's carriages are: an item with a picture, a hold, a speed. You build it
         at a shipyard from parts and launch it. Recommended. The engine already moves vehicles, seats riders in
         them, gives them cargo and saves them; what is new is that these run on water instead of rails.
      B. A ship is built of blocks, any shape, and the blocks move. Far more work (moving a piece of the world is
         something the engine cannot do), and it would be the whole mod.
    Recommended: A, with ships that are customised by what is fitted to them (sails, hold, guns, a figurehead)
    in slots, so that two brigs are not the same brig.

Q2. HOW BIG THE SEA IS. Today a world is mostly land with sea at the edges. Recommended: a world option, "Seas",
    from the game's own (as now) through Archipelago (half sea, many islands) to Ocean World (nearly all sea) -
    and the mod's content appears in whatever sea a world has, so an old world's little ocean still gets wrecks
    and reefs when it is next generated... which it never is. So also: a SEA CHART item that leads to a new place,
    "the Far Seas", made the way Dimensions makes its places: an ocean a world wide, reached by sailing off the
    edge of the home sea. Old worlds get everything that way. Say if you would rather it were only the option.

Q3. TIDES AND WEATHER AT SEA. Recommended: yes to both, modestly - the sea level rises and falls a few blocks
    twice a day (uncovering tide pools, wrecks and cave mouths), and storms at sea are dangerous to small boats
    (waves that wash a character overboard, lightning that strikes masts). Alternative: neither; a flat calm sea.

Q4. FIGHTING AT SEA. Pirates, sea serpents, a kraken. Recommended: ship's guns that fire at what is clicked on
    (they are the bow, bigger), creatures and ships that attack the hull, a hull with its own health that is
    mended with planks and pitch. No boarding, no crews with minds of their own.

Q5. A CREW. Recommended: your own characters only (the game allows several); a second character can be left at
    the helm or the guns. No hired sailors in the first version.

Q6. WITH OTHER MODS. It depends on none. Where Rags to Riches is on, harbour villages; where Culinary Delights
    is on, its recipes can take the new fish. By lists that skip what is not there.

Q7. DELIVERY. Recommended: four stages, each playable - (1) boats and the sea made bigger; (2) fishing, diving,
    reefs and wrecks; (3) big ships, guns, pirates and monsters; (4) the Far Seas, the trench, the submarine.

================================================================================================================
1. SHIPS
================================================================================================================

Built at a SHIPYARD (a bench that must stand at the water's edge), launched onto water, sailed with the movement
keys, moored at a BOLLARD. A ship has a hull (health), a draught (how deep the water must be), a speed that the
wind helps or hinders, a hold, and fittings.

   RAFT            four logs and rope. One rider, slow, no hold, capsizes in a storm. The first hour.
   ROWING BOAT     two riders, a small hold, shallow draught: goes up rivers.
   SLOOP           one mast. The first real ship: a hold of 16, a cabin with a bed (so a ship is a home).
   FISHING SMACK   a sloop's hull with a net winch and a fish hold that keeps a catch fresh.
   BRIG            two masts, a hold of 48, four gun ports, room for a workbench below.
   GALLEON         three masts, a hold of 96, twelve gun ports, a ship's furnace and kitchen.
   IRONCLAD        steam and plate: ignores the wind, burns coal, shrugs off small guns. (Tier with Industry's age.)
   DIVING TENDER   carries a diving bell on a crane and an air pump.
   SUBMARINE       goes under. Air for a while, a lamp, a claw to pick things up, a hatch to swim out of.

FITTINGS (slots on a ship): sails (linen, canvas, silk - speed), a keel (stability), a hold (bigger, or cold),
anchor, lanterns, a figurehead (each a small boon: luck with fish, calm in storms, speed), guns, a harpoon, a
net winch, a crane, a crow's nest (see further: uncovers fog of war at range), a ship's bell, a flag.

THE WIND. A direction and a strength that change through the day, shown by a pennant. With it, fast; across it,
fair; against it, a crawl. A compass and a weather glass tell you what is coming.

================================================================================================================
2. THE SEA ITSELF
================================================================================================================

Deeper, and in layers: SHALLOWS (light, reefs, seagrass), the OPEN SEA (shoals of fish, the shadow of something
big), the TWILIGHT (dim; jellyfish that glow, squid), and the ABYSS (dark; vents, bone fields, the trench).
Pressure: below a depth a swimmer takes harm without a suit, and below another without a better one.

PLACES, made by the structure system and by biomes of the sea floor:
   coral reefs in six colours (living blocks that grow, slowly, and die out of water)   kelp forests
   sandbars and atolls with a lagoon inside        sea stacks and arches        sea caves that the tide opens
   SHIPWRECKS in five sizes, each with a hold to salvage, some with a ghost in them
   sunken temples (a puzzle of flooded rooms: pump one out to open the next)     a drowned village
   hydrothermal vents (heat, and ores found nowhere else)       whale falls (bone, and what feeds on it)
   icebergs in cold seas (mostly under water; fresh water and trapped things)    a maelstrom (pulls ships in)
   lighthouses on headlands (ruined; rebuild one and the fog of war at sea lifts for a long way round it)
   pirate coves        a ghost ship that sails on its own on moonless nights        buoys and sea marks

TIDES (Q3) and CURRENTS: bands of sea that carry a ship along - the trade routes, once charted.

CHARTING. The sea is fog until sailed. A CHART TABLE turns what a character has seen into charts: an item that
shows a region to whoever holds it, that can be copied, sold, or bought from a harbour. Treasure maps are charts
with a cross on them.

================================================================================================================
3. FISHING, DIVING, SALVAGE
================================================================================================================

FISHING. Rod and line from the shore or a boat; nets and pots left out and hauled later; a harpoon for the big
ones. About 60 fish and shellfish, each of a depth, a sea and a season, in sizes (the engine already has sized
creatures); a record of the biggest of each kind caught; bait and lures that choose what bites. Fish spoil
unless salted, smoked, iced or kept alive in a tank. Pearls from oysters, a few of them black.

DIVING. Breath is the game's own. Then: a snorkel, a diving helmet fed by a hand pump on the boat (a hose: you
can go as far as it reaches), a diving bell (air pocket lowered on a chain - a base on the sea floor), a
standard dress for the twilight, a hard suit for the abyss, and at last gills from something found down there.
Under water: a lamp, a spear gun, a pry bar for wrecks, lifting bags that float a heavy thing to the surface.

SALVAGE. Wrecks hold cargo by their age (amphorae, coin chests, cannon, silver bars, a ship's figurehead to fit
to your own), sunken temples hold relics. An UNDERWATER HABITAT - glass and brass blocks that keep the sea out
when sealed and pumped - is the late-game base.

================================================================================================================
4. DANGERS
================================================================================================================

About 55 creatures. Harmless: shoals, turtles, dolphins that swim alongside, seals, gulls that show where fish
are, whales that sound. Dangerous: jellyfish (sting), barracuda, morays in the reef, sharks in five kinds,
giant squid that take hold of a hull, sea serpents, sirens on rocks (the view is pulled toward them), a KRAKEN
(a boss in several parts: arms that must be cut before the body rises), the LEVIATHAN in the trench.
PIRATES: sloops and brigs that close and fire; a pirate cove to raid; a bounty board at harbours.
THE SEA ITSELF: storms, waterspouts, fog banks, ice, whirlpools, running aground on a falling tide.

================================================================================================================
5. WHAT IT MAKES
================================================================================================================

Materials: salt, pitch, tar, canvas, oakum, rope and cable, ship's timbers, copper sheathing, brass, whalebone,
ambergris, pearls and mother-of-pearl, coral, sea glass, sponge, shark skin, squid ink, deep-sea ores (three).
Benches: shipyard (5 grades), sail loft, ropewalk, smokehouse, salt pans (work by the sun), chart table,
net loft, cooperage (barrels: the sea's chests), diving workshop, brass foundry.
Building: dock pilings, piers, jetties, slipways, sea walls, lock gates (a canal with working locks), cranes,
lighthouse lamp, harbour lights, portholes, hammocks, ship's wheels as furniture, figureheads, nets hung to dry.
Food: forty dishes of fish. Clothing: oilskins (storms), a sou'wester, sea boots, a captain's coat and hat,
diving dress - with set bonuses.
Trade: cargo runs. A harbour buys what it lacks at a premium; a hold full of the right thing is a fortune, and
a storm on the way is the risk. (This is the mod's money, and it is the reason to build a bigger ship.)

LONG MAKINGS, as the owner likes them: the galleon is about fourteen makings (timber -> seasoned -> frames ->
keel -> hull -> ... -> rigged), the submarine and the hard suit about sixteen, each needing the ship before it.

================================================================================================================
6. ENGINE
================================================================================================================

New, as data fields any mod could use:
   - vehicles that run on water (a kind of vehicle beside rails): draught, wind, hull health, fittings as a
     container with typed slots; mooring; riders who can walk about on deck (a vehicle with a floor);
   - sea level as a number that changes (tides) rather than a constant; currents as a field over water;
   - wind (direction, strength) as world state, usable by anything (windmills would like it);
   - pressure by depth (the opposite of cold by height); air supplied through a hose or a sealed room - "sealed
     room" being a flood-fill the game does not have yet, and that a space mod would also want;
   - things that spoil over time (a number kept with a stack, as meters are);
   - creatures of several parts (the kraken); creatures that attack vehicles; vehicles that are hostile;
   - a fishing action (cast, wait, strike) and catch tables by depth, biome, season, bait;
   - charts: an item that carries a piece of the explored map.
Reused: vehicles and riding (trains), sized creatures and swimming (fish, sharks), structures (villages),
dimensions (the Far Seas), effects (wet, seasick, the bends), relic slots (figureheads), fog of war.

HARD PARTS, said now: walking on a moving deck; water that looks right around a hull; the tide moving a great
deal of water every few minutes without slowing the game (it must be a level, not a simulation); the kraken.
