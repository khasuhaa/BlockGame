PROPOSAL 3 - THE UNDERCROFT   (a dungeon and combat mod; MAJOR; for approval - nothing is built)

Fighting in this game is clicking on a creature until it stops. Nothing is built to be fought through. This mod
adds the two things together: weapons that are used differently from one another, enemies that do more than walk
at you - and places made to put the two to the test. Dungeons that are different every time, that go down floor
by floor, that you can lose in, and that send you home with something worth the trip.

    a cellar door in a ruin  ->  a crypt of five floors  ->  a keep  ->  a labyrinth that moves  ->
    the Undercroft itself: a hundred floors, and a ladder of other people's best runs to climb

Nothing else in the game or its mods is about combat as a skill, or about a place you enter to be tested.
Size: ~300 blocks, ~650 items, ~600 recipes, ~90 enemies, 12 bosses.

================================================================================================================
0. QUESTIONS
================================================================================================================

Q1. HOW MUCH THE FIGHTING CHANGES. Recommended: a real change, for everyone, whenever the mod is on -
      - weapons have a REACH, an ARC and a WIND-UP: a dagger is fast and close, a spear long and narrow, a
        hammer slow and wide, a bow and a sling at range, a whip through gaps;
      - a BLOCK (hold: a shield or a two-handed weapon stops what is in front) and a DODGE (a short dash through);
      - enemies TELEGRAPH: a pose before a blow, an outline on the squares it will hit;
      - STAMINA is spent by swinging, blocking and dodging (the game has an energy bar: this is a faster one).
    The game is played by clicking where to go, not by holding keys, so all of this must work by clicks: click a
    creature to attack with what is in hand; hold to block; a key to dodge. Say if you would rather the base
    game's combat stayed exactly as it is and only dungeon enemies were smarter.

Q2. WHAT IS LOST BY DYING IN ONE. Recommended: what was FOUND on this run, and nothing brought in. A dungeon is
    a gamble with its own stakes. Harder modes (lose what you carried) as a choice at the door.

Q3. WHERE DUNGEONS ARE. Recommended: each is a place of its own, made when its door is opened, the way the
    World Seed makes its pocket world - so they can be any size, different every time, and gone when left.
    Doors are found in the world (cellars under ruins, a crack in a cave wall, a drowned stair) and can be
    carried home and set up, so a favourite kind of dungeon is always to hand.

Q4. ALONE OR TOGETHER. The game lets a player have several characters. Recommended: a party of up to three goes
    in together; the ones not being played follow and fight by simple rules (stay near, hit what is hit).

Q5. THE LADDER. "Other people's best runs" needs other people. Recommended for now: a ladder of YOUR OWN runs
    and a few built-in ghosts to beat, kept with the playset; no network.

Q6. DELIVERY. Recommended: four stages - (1) the new fighting, and enemies to try it on above ground;
    (2) the dungeon maker, the crypt, loot; (3) the other four kinds of dungeon, bosses; (4) the Undercroft,
    classes, the ladder.

================================================================================================================
1. WEAPONS AND FIGHTING
================================================================================================================

TWELVE KINDS OF WEAPON, each in the game's metals (and any mod's): dagger, sword, greatsword, axe, hammer,
spear, halberd, mace and shield, whip, bow, crossbow, sling - and staves that throw what Magic and Mayhem's
scrolls throw, where that mod is on. Each kind has two moves: a plain one and a held one (a lunge, a sweep, a
charged shot, a shield bash).

WHAT A BLOW DOES besides harm: knocks back, staggers, bleeds, burns, freezes, poisons, stuns, disarms, pulls,
pins. Enemies have weaknesses and armour by kind: bone shrugs off arrows and breaks under a hammer; slime
takes fire; plate wants a mace or a gap found by a dagger from behind. Reading the room is the skill.

ARMOUR in three weights, which trade protection for how far a dodge goes and how fast stamina comes back.
TRINKETS, in the relic slots: one rule-bending effect each ("your dodge leaves fire", "blocking a blow at the
last moment turns it back"). A build is a weapon, a weight, and four trinkets.

CLASSES, late and optional (Q6): six of them, each a short tree of things learned by doing - the Warden
(shield), the Reaver (two-handed), the Skirmisher (dodge and dagger), the Ranger, the Trapper (lays what
dungeons lay), the Chanter (songs that mark, slow and embolden; the party's support).

================================================================================================================
2. DUNGEONS
================================================================================================================

HOW ONE IS MADE. From ROOMS drawn by hand (the structure system, a few hundred of them) joined by rule: a way
through from door to stair, side rooms off it, a locked room with its key elsewhere, a secret behind a wall
that sounds hollow, a shrine, a shop, a boss. Each kind of dungeon has its own rooms, enemies, traps and rule.

FIVE KINDS, AND THE LAST:
   THE CRYPT        The dead. Short, straight, dark - torches matter. The one that teaches.
   THE KEEP         Soldiers who call for help, portcullises, arrow slits, a siege engine to turn round.
   THE WARREN       Dug by something. No rooms: tunnels that close behind you. Swarms. A queen.
   THE WORKS        A flooded factory of traps: pistons, crushers, steam (Machinations' parts, where it is on;
                    its own otherwise). Few enemies. The dungeon is the enemy.
   THE LABYRINTH    Walls that move on a count. A thing in it that cannot be killed, only avoided, until the end.
   THE UNDERCROFT   All of them, a hundred floors, a boss every ten, a rest every five. Deeper is harder
                    without end. How far you got is the score.

IN EVERY DUNGEON: keys and locks in three colours; pressure plates, levers, pushable blocks (the engine has
all of these); pits, darts, flame jets, falling ceilings, floors that give way; chests, some of them mimics;
barrels to break; shrines that offer a bargain (a boon for the run, at a price for the run); a merchant who
takes what the dungeon pays in; a map that fills in as you go.

A RUN'S OWN POWERS. Besides what you carry, a run gathers BLESSINGS - temporary, stacking, gone at the door:
choose one of three after each boss. Two runs with the same gear play differently. (The effects engine that
Potioncraft brought is what these are.)

================================================================================================================
3. ENEMIES AND BOSSES
================================================================================================================

About 90, in families of the dungeon they belong to, each family with a grunt, an archer, a brute, a caster, a
swarm and an elite - and each with ONE thing it does that must be answered: the shield-bearer who must be
flanked, the bomber who must be killed far away, the healer who must be killed first, the mimic, the one that
splits, the one that only moves when unseen. Enemies work together by simple rules (archers keep back, brutes
go first, some flee to fetch others).

TWELVE BOSSES, each a room built for it and a fight in phases: the Bone King who raises what you killed on the
way in; the Castellan on the wall, fought up a siege tower; the Brood Mother and the collapsing nest; the
Foreman - a machine with four arms that are four machines; the Minotaur, who is the Labyrinth's unkillable
thing, killable at last by the walls themselves; and seven more down the Undercroft.

ABOVE GROUND, so that the new fighting matters outside: bandit camps, a wandering knight who duels and yields
his sword, night raids on a base that has grown rich (a setting; off by default).

================================================================================================================
4. WHAT COMES OUT
================================================================================================================

Dungeon coin (spent at the dungeon's merchant and at a QUARTERMASTER set up at home); weapons and armour with
ONE random property each from a list of sixty (found, never made - the reason to go back); trinkets;
materials found nowhere else, for the top tier of each weapon kind; recipes for the home benches; trophies
(a boss's head for the wall); room blueprints, with which to build a dungeon of your own at home and send a
friend's character - or your second one - through it.

Benches: armoury (5 grades), fletcher, whetstone and repair bench (weapons wear), trophy mount, the
quartermaster, a training yard (dummies that fight back by a script you set: practise a boss).

================================================================================================================
5. ENGINE
================================================================================================================

New, as data fields:
   - attacks with shape and timing (reach, arc, wind-up, recovery) for characters and creatures; hit squares
     shown before a blow lands; block, dodge, stagger, knock-back; a stamina bar;
   - creature behaviours as small scripts in data (approach, keep distance, flee and fetch, guard a place,
     phases by health) instead of the handful of fixed kinds there are now - the biggest single gain for every
     other mod as well;
   - a dungeon maker: rooms joined by rule, with locks and keys placed so that they can always be solved;
   - places that are made on entering and thrown away on leaving (a pocket world that is not kept);
   - followers: your other characters acting by rule when not played;
   - random properties on an item (a number kept with it, as meters are);
   - a run: what was gained since the door, forfeited on death.
Reused: structures (rooms), pocket places (Dimensions), signals and pistons (traps, moving walls), effects
(blessings, bleeding, burning), relic slots (trinkets), sets, cages (a captured mimic is a chest that bites).

HARD PARTS: combat by clicks that still feels like skill (Q1) - this needs a prototype before the rest is
worth building; locks and keys that are never unsolvable; followers that are a help and not a nuisance; ninety
enemies that are ninety things and not six things in fifteen colours.
